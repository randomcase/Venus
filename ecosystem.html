The Ecosystem

Nine thousand square miles of forest, the size of England, and it is not scenery — it is the machine that makes this planet habitable. Twelve orders live in it. Take one out and see what stops working — and what carries on regardless, which is usually the more interesting half.

The twelve orders each one has a job, none of them is decoration

Everything above the last two was air. The fireflies, moths, butterflies and bees work in the space above the ground, the owls fly it, the wolves cross it and the ants draw on it — and until the beetles, nothing lived in it. A forest is not its canopy. The canopy is what the floor is currently spending, and a wood with nothing working the litter layer is running down a balance it has no way to top up. The spiders take the other empty place, which is the gap between things: the only order here whose habitat is the space itself.

And two that are not animals made on purpose, and not required

Every order above earned its job over a very long time; nothing chose them and nothing designed them, and each is here because the wood kept what worked. These two are not that. There was a ship, it failed, and what was left of it came ashore in a forest that was managing perfectly well. The wolves raised them, which is both the oldest version of this story and the literal arrangement on this page. They are loved by nobody, they are not beautiful, and Terraform does not list them — the forest was doing that before they arrived and would carry on if they stopped. They are the only pair here whose success is not yet known. They keep trying, every time, which is the entire case for them and the page is not going to oversell it.

Untick one and watch. The capabilities below are not a list of features, they are conjunctions: each one names the orders it actually requires, and it disappears when any of them leaves. Twenty-one rules for twenty-one capabilities — adding a thirteenth animal costs as many rules as it has jobs, not a doubled table.

What the forest can do counted from the panels, not declared

Keep timeneeds fireflies

There is a calendar. Everything else in this wood can be scheduled, compared, replayed and argued about, because there is an agreed answer to when. It cost one signal per flash and no memory of anybody else.

The clock is the cheapest thing in the forest and the only one nothing else can substitute for. See the Pulse for how it settles, and the lessons for how to build one.

Decideneeds owls · fireflies

A quorum can be called, a question can be put, and the answer binds. Note what it requires: not just the owls but the clock, because a vote with no agreed when is nine opinions in a wood rather than a decision.

See the Bench of Nine for what happens when you keep adding judges to fix it, and the Pulse for a chamber that votes on a circuit.

Moveneeds wolves

Something in this wood can act inside a day. It needs no clock and no quorum, which is exactly why it is dangerous and exactly why it is indispensable — an ecosystem of nothing but deliberators does not get slower, it stops.

Proveneeds peacocks · owls

A claim can be made in a form that costs the claimant something, and there is somebody whose job is to read it. Both halves are required. A costly signal with nobody assessing it is just an expensive animal, and an assessor with no costly signal to read is taking everybody's word.

See the flowneeds flamingos · fireflies

The wood has an instrument. You can tell what is moving through it by looking at what the flamingos are made of — and it needs the clock, because a reading with no timestamp is a colour rather than a measurement.

Spreadneeds bees

The forest can reach past where it started. Two trees on opposite sides of a valley are rooted, cannot travel, and will never meet — and the bees make them neighbours anyway. This is the only capability in the wood that is about coverage rather than about function, and terraforming a planet is very largely a coverage problem.

The waggle dance is worth its own line: a bee that has found something returns and dances an angle against the vertical and a duration, and other bees fly to a place they have never seen on the strength of a symbol. That is not signalling that something is good. That is saying where, in an abstraction, to somebody who was not there.

Inheritneeds butterflies

Something in this wood can hold a route that is longer than any life on it. Nobody flying it will see both ends. The generation that arrives is three or four removed from the one that left, they never met, and the way is kept anyway — not written down, not taught, and not dependent on anybody surviving the trip.

The metamorphosis belongs here too, and it is the less sentimental half: a body that dissolves and reassembles as a different animal, with a different food and a different medium, is a system becoming something else without starting again from nothing. Every other order in this wood can only ever be a better version of itself.

Recoverneeds bees · butterflies · fireflies

The forest can come back from a loss. All three are required and none is sufficient: the bees carry the material to where it is needed, the butterflies hold the route across generations that will not overlap, and the fireflies supply the when that makes it a recovery rather than an accident.

Note which orders are not on this list. Coming back does not require anybody to decide it, prove it, report it or move quickly. It requires carriage, memory and a clock, and it happens on a timescale where none of the four louder orders would even register that it had begun.

Pathneeds ants

The wood finds good routes with nobody holding the map. A trail is walked, walking reinforces it, and the reinforcement makes it likelier to be walked again — and no ant on it has ever seen more of the wood than the metre in front of its face.

The half that matters is the evaporation. A trail nobody renews fades on its own, so a route that has stopped being worth walking stops existing without anybody having to notice it, decide about it, or remove it. This is the only structure in the forest that forgets by default, and it is the reason the ground can be used as memory without silting up.

Hold a bearingneeds moths

Something can fly a straight line across a dark wood. The rule is one line: keep a constant angle to a distant light. It works because the moon does not move relative to you as you travel, so the angle you are holding is a direction.

And the same rule, unaltered, puts them in the candle. A light six feet away does change angle as you move, so holding it constant produces a spiral rather than a line. The moth is not malfunctioning. It is a heuristic calibrated to a condition, behaving exactly as specified, in a condition that no longer holds — which is the most common way a working system fails, and the hardest to see from inside it. Every capability on this page has a version of it.

Navigateneeds moths · fireflies · ants

Not a bearing — a fix. Three independent references, and none of them enough alone: a distant light for direction, a clock for elapsed time, and a trail on the ground to correct against. That is what navigation has always actually been, and it is why one reference is a heuristic and three is a position.

Take the ants away and the wood still holds a bearing and still knows the hour, and it has no way to notice it has been flying at a candle for a mile. The third reference is not redundancy. It is the only thing that can disagree with the other two.

Catchneeds spiders

Coverage without patrol. Every other order that watches anything has to go and look; a web watches an entire volume of air continuously, reports the moment anything crosses it, and costs almost nothing during the long stretches when nothing does. It is the cheapest surveillance in the forest by an enormous margin, and the reason is that the structure does the watching rather than the animal.

And it cannot be worn while its owner is growing. A spider moults by shedding a shell it has outgrown, and it cannot do that inside the web — it leaves, hangs somewhere undefended, comes out soft, and waits to harden before it can go back. It does not become a different animal the way the beetle and the butterfly do; it is the same design, larger. But there is no version of this where it rebuilds itself inside the thing it is serving from, and no way to make the window shorter. Every system that has ever had to ship a new version of itself has met this problem and none of them enjoyed it.

Buryneeds beetles

Seed goes down. Not sideways — down, to a depth where it is out of reach of the things that eat it and in reach of the things that feed it. The bees are the network and this is the write: carriage decides where a seed arrives, and burial decides whether arriving was worth anything.

Burial is imprecise, slow and done by force, which is why it is easy to mistake for the unimportant half. A wood that can carry seed everywhere and bury none of it has excellent logistics and no next generation.

And the beetles are what the top of the wood learns on. A young eagle hunts beetles — slow, armoured, abundant, on the ground, and forgiving of a bad first attempt. The apex of this forest does not begin at the apex. It begins on the floor, taking the easiest thing there is, and the floor is what makes that possible. This is the only place on the page where the orders are shown eating each other rather than depending on each other, and it is worth one line because a capability graph will never show it.

The armour is a pair of wings. A beetle's elytra are its forewings, hardened into a roof over the flight wings underneath, so it does not carry protection in addition to the ability to move — it carries one structure that can be either, and it must open the shell to use the wings. There is no configuration where it is both armoured and airborne. That is the same shape as the spider that cannot moult in its own web, and it is the honest version of every trade-off between being safe and being able to change: you do not get to pay for both at the same instant, and the moment you are changing is exactly the moment you are open.

Tell elapsed timeneeds beetles

You can tell the time by a beetle on the forest floor, and not loosely. The succession that works a fallen thing arrives in a known order, so how far along it has got and who is currently on it reads out as a duration. Done deliberately this is forensic entomology; done by the wood it is simply the floor keeping count.

So there are two clocks here and they are not spare copies of each other. The fireflies keep the fast one: its division is a cycle, its unit is tonight, and everything that has to be scheduled or agreed runs on it. The floor keeps the slow one: its division is a season, its unit is a decade, and nothing can be scheduled on it at all. What it can do is answer how long has it been when the fast clock is gone — which is the difference between a wood that has lost its clock and a wood that has lost time.

Retrieveneeds geckos

An arbitrary place, one specific thing, and back. Everything else here moves in patterns — bees run routes between fixed points, beetles work downward, ants follow the trail they are already on, spiders wait for the world to come to them. This is the only order that goes and gets, and the only one whose job is a single item rather than a flow.

The chameleon does the same job without travelling: a ballistic tongue that reaches further than the animal is long, and two eyes running independent searches that converge only when one of them finds something. The tree frog is that animal with the going taken out — sit high, hold still, grow, hum, and let the reach do the work.

And the tail comes off on purpose. Autotomy is not an injury, it is a decision: a deliberate partial loss to keep the whole, taken instantly and without consultation. It grows back as cartilage rather than vertebrae — functional, and not the original. Every system that has shed a component to stay up has made this trade and has told itself the same thing about restoring it properly later.

Go unseenneeds geckos

The wood contains something that can be present and not observed. Look at what that is set against: the peacocks exist to be seen and pay for it, the flamingos are readable by construction, the fireflies literally advertise, the ants write down everywhere they have been, and the spiders turn a whole volume of air into notification. Eleven orders are instruments. One is not.

The chameleon is not how this works, whatever everyone says. Chameleons change colour mainly to signal — threat, courtship, submission — and to manage heat. It is speech and thermostat, not concealment; the concealment is what their ordinary resting colour was already doing. Filing them under hiding mistakes an animal talking for an animal disappearing, and that mistake generalises further than it looks.

This is also the only capability here that is worth having and worth worrying about, and the page will not pretend otherwise. A forest with no way to move unobserved cannot recover anything from anywhere dangerous. A forest where the only unobservable order is also the only retriever has put both in one animal, which is a concentration nobody designed and nobody voted for.

Salvageneeds geckos · ants

A gecko can go anywhere and bring back one thing, and it has no idea where anything is. The trail knows where. Neither half is any use alone: retrieval without a location is an animal wandering, and a location with nothing that can reach it is a note about somewhere you cannot go.

It is the only pairing in the wood between the order that marks everything it touches and the order that marks nothing. The map is drawn by the ones who cannot be discreet and used by the one who cannot be followed.

Noticeneeds spiders · owls

Something crossed the wood and somebody read the report. Both halves are required and neither is any use alone: a web with nobody assessing what it caught is an instrument logging into a void, and an assessor with no instrument is asking around. This is the same shape as Prove — a signal and a reader — and the wood needs both shapes, because one handles claims that were made to it and the other handles things that simply happened.

Build soilneeds beetles · ants

The floor is turned rather than merely accumulating. Beetles break the litter down and take it under; ants move it around and open it up. Between them the ground stops being a pile of what fell and starts being the thing the next century of forest is made out of.

This is the slowest capability on the page and the only one whose absence is invisible for decades. Nothing breaks when it stops. The wood simply becomes, over a very long time, a thinner version of itself, and no single year is ever the year it went wrong.

Repairneeds Romulus · Remus

Something that broke can be worked on. Not fixed — worked on. This is the only capability on the page that describes an attempt rather than a function, and the only one whose entry could be removed tomorrow without any other capability noticing.

It needs both of them and there is no version with one. Romulus is a set with one member; Remus is the complement, which is everything else. A wallet on its own is a balance — a number that is true and says nothing about whether anything is getting better. The complement on its own cannot be totalled at all: there is no operation that counts everything that is not one thing. Put them together and you have an account, and an account is the only instrument that can tell you whether a repair is working or whether you are simply spending at a rate nobody has looked at.

The split between them was a draw, not a judgement — three brothers once divided the world by lot and the one who got the part underneath did not get it for being worse. The reason that matters here is what comes next: the thing gets named after whoever held the countable half, the division gets re-explained afterwards as merit, and nobody will ever admit why ninety per cent of anything ended up one way rather than the other.

Terraformneeds all twelve

The full machine. It keeps time, decides, acts, proves what it did, shows what passed through it, spreads past the clearing it began in, and carries the route across lives that do not overlap — which between them is the whole of what making a planet habitable actually consists of — along with the ground to write routes on, a bearing to cross it by, a floor that is being made rather than spent, and an instrument strung across the gap — and none of it is the dramatic part.

Nine thousand square miles and twelve orders, and the two that matter most to whether any of it is still here in a century are the two smallest animals in the wood: the one that keeps the time and the one that keeps the way.

The clock is goneno fireflies

Look at what is still lit above, because it is not nothing and that is the whole point. The wolves still move. If the peacocks and owls are both here, claims can still be proved. What has stopped is everything that required agreement: nothing can be scheduled, the quorum cannot be called, and the flamingos are a colour rather than a reading.

And if the beetles are here, the wood still knows roughly how long it has been. The floor is the second clock: no use for scheduling anything, since its smallest division is a season, but it answers how long when nothing else can. A wood in this state has lost its clock. It has not lost time. The bees also carry on, which is worth noticing: the forest keeps spreading while it is unable to decide anything about where. So losing the clock does not stop the forest. It stops the deliberate half and leaves the fast half running — a wood that can still act and can no longer decide, still signal and no longer schedule. That is a worse condition than a wood that has stopped, and it looks considerably healthier from outside.

This page originally claimed that losing the fireflies darkened everything, and enforced it with a blanket rule at the bottom of the stylesheet. The rule lost, silently, to every capability that named two orders — because those selectors carry more specificity and cascade order only settles ties. The comment at the foot of the stylesheet keeps the arithmetic. The claim was wrong before the rule was; fixing the rule would only have made a false statement enforceable.

Bare rock nothing present

No orders at all. This is what the planet was before the forest, and it is the state the page opens into if you clear every box — designed for deliberately rather than fallen into, because a system with nothing sensible to say about being empty is not finished.

Why these twelve and not twelve nicer ones

Every animal here does a real job that the others cannot, and each job is a real mechanism rather than a mood. Fireflies genuinely synchronise by pulse coupling and it genuinely needs no leader. Owls are a committee and committees genuinely fail by abstention. A peacock's train is the standard example of costly signalling, and the reason it works is that it is wasteful — the waste is load-bearing. A flamingo really is pink because of what it eats, which makes it the rarest thing in any system: an instrument that cannot be configured to say something convenient.

Wolves are two. They were always two. They are not here to be defeated; they are here because a wood with no velocity in it is a wood that deliberates its way to a stop, and because an audit that has never been tested against anybody quick is not an audit, it is a habit.

The bees and the butterflies were added last and they are the two the wood would miss longest. Everything the other five do, they do now: this decision, this claim, this reading, this hour. Carriage and inheritance are the only two jobs in the forest whose whole value is measured after the animal doing them is gone — a tree pollinated by a bee that died in the summer, a route flown by four generations of an insect that never met each other. A wood with the five and not the last two works perfectly and ends with the animals currently in it.

The ants and the moths are the two that are about being wrong. An ant trail is a claim about the world that decays unless the world keeps agreeing with it, which is the only self-correcting memory in the wood. A moth is a rule that is right for a thousand generations and then meets a candle, which is the only honest picture anywhere on this page of how a good system actually fails: not by breaking, but by continuing to work perfectly in a condition it was never calibrated for.

The spiders and the beetles are the two the wood would notice last and miss most. A web is the only instrument here that watches an area instead of a point and charges nothing for the hours when nothing happens. Burial is the only service that decides whether carriage was worth doing. And between them they hold the two places every other order left empty — the floor, and the gap. They are also the two that state the same law from opposite ends: a spider cannot moult inside the web it is running, and a beetle cannot fly with its armour shut. Being protected and being able to change are the same budget, and nothing in this wood gets to spend it twice. The floor is not the boring part of a forest. The canopy is what the floor is currently spending.
