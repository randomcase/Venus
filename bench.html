The Bench of Nine

Nine thousand square miles of forest — the size of England — and one court in it. In re the Pot, first case. Two votes are taken here and the first one is not about the pot. There are seventeen judges and three clergy; never more than fifteen of the judges vote, one never does, one will not sign anything, one is paid by the disposal — and the three who write what binds are named nowhere in the record. It is still called the Bench of Nine. no script †

In re the Pot on appeal from the forest floor

Nine came together for one thing, and the thing was food. Money went in the pot. The pot was split, dashed and dined. The court below held that the pot was a vote — that paying in was consent, that everyone who dined had voted, and that the one who has abstained for fifteen years consented by saying nothing.

The appeal is narrow and the page thinks it is right. Matriculating funds upward is not voting. A structure that takes money in, promotes from within, and reproduces itself in bodies that are not identical but identitarianly aligned has no step at which anybody is asked — and holding that the pot was the asking is precisely how it never adds one. Abstention is not consent. Fifteen years of it is not fifteen years of agreement; it is fifteen years of a question nobody put.

How the case comes up three keys, and they are all you hold

The grant — what is put before the bench
The season — whether the canopy is closed
The ninth chair — whether the abstainer is asked to sit

Nobody's mind changes when you move these. What moves is which of nine practised positions each judge is standing in when the question arrives — which is what a practice is, and why a bench is not an opinion poll.

Certiorari granted

Four or more, on every one of the twelve circuits. The forest is nine thousand square miles and this court hears eighty things a year; getting through that door is most of what winning is, and on this board it is no longer possible to fail at it.

It was possible until the twelfth seat. Four circuits used to end here — papers or the pot, with the ninth chair empty — and on those four the judgment below stood unreviewed and unreasoned, and the pot remained a vote in every county of a jurisdiction the size of England, not because a court held that it was but because no court held anything. Three seats were added to repair this bench. The first two were barred from this vote and changed nothing about it. The third was not, and it changed everything about it, immediately, on her first sitting. Denial is not disagreement and it was never a small thing; it was simply the number the fewest people ever looked at.

The bench three benches of three, and then six more

0Rowanchief justice · the fifteenth seat
Seated above the bench rather than at the end of it, and she takes no part in either vote — she does not grant and she does not decide. Not one number on this page moved when she arrived: the same eight circuits reverse, the same two affirm, the same two deadlock on the pot, and every judge here votes exactly as they did the day before. What she has is the assignment. Until her appointment the pen went to the Chief when the Chief was in the majority and otherwise to the most senior judge who was, so Hawthorn wrote eight of the ten decided circuits and Rook wrote two. Rowan is never in the majority, because she never votes, and under that rule she could not have assigned anything at all — so the rule was changed with her. She assigns all ten. No outcome changed. Every word of every judgment did.

10Yewcoppice · the tenth seat
Seated on this bench to stop it deadlocking, which is the reason a tenth seat has ever been added to any bench anywhere. Ten is an even number. She votes with the ninth chair and never against a majority that already exists — seated, Wren has company and Yew reverses; recused, Wren is absent and Yew affirms the court below. She grants nothing: seated to decide, not to select, so the rule of four is the one vote here she takes no part in. The two four-four splits are gone. 11Alderwindthrow · the eleventh seat
Seated because a ten-judge court kept tying, which is what happens when you repair an odd bench by making it even. Eleven is odd again and nothing on this board ties any more. She votes with the season: in leaf she affirms, bare she reverses. She grants nothing either — two judges have now been added to this court to fix it, and neither of them has any say in whether it hears anything at all. Both five-five splits are gone. Both went the same way when she broke them, and it was not the appellant's way.
grants on: never
12Brackenthe rolls · the twelfth seat
The first seat ever added to this bench that votes on whether there is a case. Every previous repair was barred from the certiorari vote, which is why none of them moved the door an inch. She grants always — a court that hears eighty things a year in a jurisdiction the size of England should be embarrassed by that number and not comforted by it — and the count now clears four on all twelve circuits. She reverses on the merits, having read the file, which is her job. Twelve is even, so the ties are back, on exactly the two circuits they were on before. This time they are heard.
grants on: always
13Sedgestanding proxy · the thirteenth seat
Holds the ninth chair's proxy and nothing else. When Wren is recused, Sedge votes as Wren votes, on the merits and on certiorari both; when Wren is seated the proxy is void and Sedge casts nothing, because a proxy substitutes for a person rather than adding to a bench. Sitting therefore reads twelve on every circuit, which is even on every circuit. Giving the absent woman a voice did not resolve anything — it doubled the deadlocks, from two to four. The two circuits that used to affirm while she was away now tie instead. They tie because the court is genuinely split down the middle on the question, and her absence is what had been keeping that off the record.
grants: as she does
14Hazelof the table · the fourteenth seat
She was at the pot. She put money in and she dined, and she is the only person on this bench with first-hand knowledge of whether anybody was ever asked anything — and the only one disqualified from saying so when it counts. On the papers and on the record she sits and reverses, because she was there and nobody asked her. When the pot itself is put before the court she recuses for interest, which is the correct rule and is not in any doubt. Sitting reads thirteen on the papers and on the record, which is odd, so those eight circuits cannot tie and all eight now decide cleanly. It reads twelve on the pot, and both remaining deadlocks are there. This court can decide the case from a distance and cannot decide it with the thing in front of it, because the rule that keeps the bench clean is the rule that removes its only witness. Nobody did anything wrong. That is the problem.
grants: papers, record
16Birksplit ash · concurs in the judgment only
She votes on the result and joins no opinion, which is a real position and an honourable one: she agrees the appellant wins and she will not put her name to anybody’s reasons for it. She reverses, having read the file, and her vote counts in the division like any other — so both deadlocks on the pot are gone, decided, and decided for the appellant. But on those two circuits the majority exists only because of her, and a majority that depends on a judge who joins no opinion is not a majority opinion. It is a plurality: the case is disposed of, nobody has stated the law, and nothing binds the next court. The two circuits with the pot on the table can now be decided and cannot be explained. Hazel is recused for interest and Birk will not sign.
grants: never
17The Masterthe ordinary list · paid per disposal
He takes this question forty times a day and has done for eleven years. He is fast, he is almost always right, and he is the only reason the sixteen above him ever reach a hard case — a court that heard every pot would hear nothing else. But toggle the circuits and watch this card: it does not change. Every other judge on this bench is selected by what is in front of them; his is the one card on the board with no condition on it at all, because the file stopped being the input somewhere around the four thousandth time. And he is paid per disposal, which is the only line a list can count — so the fee reads the same on the eight circuits that produce a judgment and the two that produce a plurality with no holding in it. Nothing here is corrupt. It is an accurate meter measuring the wrong quantity, which is worse, because there is nobody to accuse.
grants: all, always

The clergy paid to assist · and the reasons are theirs

A disposal needs a judge. An opinion needs somebody to write it, and nobody on the bench above has the hours. Three clerks are retained. Between them they draft all twelve, the judges read, correct and sign them, and not one of the twelve carries a drafter’s name. Release them with the control below and watch which number falls and which does not.

Sister Wrendrafts · the merits

Reads the file the Master no longer reads, and writes the paragraph the next court will quote. Eleven years less service than he has, and the sentence that binds is hers.

Brother Alderdrafts · the dissents

Writes both sides, including the one his judge is against, because a dissent nobody drafted is a dissent nobody filed — and an unanswered majority is a stronger precedent than a contested one.

Sister Holtdrafts · the record

Keeps the citations honest and the findings tied to line numbers, which is the whole reason Exhibit A can be checked against this judgment at all.

Note also what the Master does not touch. Scroll back: Reverse, Affirm and Sitting are identical to what they were before the seventeenth seat existed, because a Master of the ordinary list is not on the appellate bench and his card increments neither. That is not an omission in the stylesheet, it is the situation. The bench of sixteen settles this question once, in public, with a holding. He settles it forty times a day, on a list, and none of it is in the court’s figures — the figures were built to count the sixteen.

Which is the finding, and it is the hardest one this board has produced. The part of a court’s output that is disposable is the part with a name on it. The part that binds the next court — the holding, the reasons, the sentence somebody will quote across nine thousand square miles of forest for the next forty years — is drafted by three people the record does not mention, on a retainer, and the meter that pays for all of it cannot see the difference between a judgment and a plurality. Nobody did anything wrong. That is the problem, and it is the second time this board has had to say so.

The judgment eight reverse, two affirm, two plurality — none tie

Reversed

The pot was not a vote. Paying in is not consent, dining is not ratification, and the fifteen-year abstention is an abstention — it says what it has always said, which is nothing, and nothing may not be read as yes. The holding below is vacated across all nine thousand square miles.

Opinion assigned by Rowan, C.J., who did not vote. Under the rule that stood until her appointment the pen would have gone to Hawthorn, J., who is in the majority and was Chief until the fifteenth seat.Rook, J., the most senior judge in a majority the old Chief was not in — the only power that fact ever gave her, and it is gone.

Affirmed

The court below stands. Put to this bench in leaf, or put to it as the pot itself, a majority of eleven will hold that the money was the meaning — and they are not fools, they are reading a structure that has genuinely never had a step where anyone was asked, and concluding that the pot must therefore have been it. That is the error. It is an extremely easy one.

Reversed · plurality · no opinion of the court

The appellant wins and the court has held nothing. Both circuits that end here are on the pot, and until the sixteenth seat they were the two deadlocks. Birk has broken them, for the appellant, and she concurs in the judgment only — she will not put her name to anybody’s reasons. Take her vote away and there is no majority at all, so the majority exists only because of a judge who joins no opinion. That is a plurality: the case is disposed of, nobody has stated the law, and nothing here binds the next court that is asked.

So on the two circuits where the pot is actually on the table, this bench can now decide and cannot explain. Hazel was at the table and must recuse for interest. Birk will not sign. Six appointments were made to repair this court, and what they have produced, at the point of closest contact with the evidence, is a result with no reasons attached to it — which is the same document the court below issued, and the same one the pot issued before that.

Why the first vote is the whole case the page being opinionated

A syndicate does not vote. It matriculates funds upward and promotes from within, and those are one motion seen from either end. Being cyclical, it reproduces — not a copy but a body identitarianly aligned with itself, mimicking the structure closely enough to be run the same way by the same hands. Less amoeba than germ: cohesive as one organism, factional in its parts, and the factions are hats rather than people. There is no love in it and no sex and no survival. It is a cycle that learned to make another of itself, and the reason it looks transparent the moment you see it is that it was never hiding — it simply has no step at which anyone is asked.

Which is why the rule of four is the real holding of this page and the merits are the footnote. Four votes is the step where somebody gets asked. Deny cert and the structure keeps running with a court sitting right on top of it, unreversed, having decided nothing — and that is not a failure of the system, it is the system working exactly as built. The one reliable way to lose In re the Pot was to leave the ninth chair empty, and the ninth chair was empty for fifteen years. Two seats have since been added to correct the court that produced that result. Neither of them votes on whether there is a case. The door is exactly where it was.

Where to start

The board opens on the papers · in leaf · recused, which is three votes to hear and therefore no case at all. Seat the ninth chair — though the door no longer closes on anyone and the ninth chair votes either way now, because the twelfth seat opened one and the thirteenth holds the other's proxy. Then put the case up on the pot, both seasons, and watch the only two deadlocks left on the board appear. Five appointments were made to fix this court. Put the case up on the pot, both seasons, and read what comes back: a result, for the appellant, with no opinion of the court attached to it. Then look at the line under any of the other judgments and note who signs them now. Six appointments. Decide for yourself which of them fixed anything, and then read the last paragraph of the source comment.
