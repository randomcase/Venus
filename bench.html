The Bench of Nine

Nine thousand square miles of forest — the size of England — and one court in it. In re the Pot, first case. Two votes are taken here and the first one is not about the pot. no script

In re the Pot on appeal from the forest floor

Nine came together for one thing, and the thing was food. Money went in the pot. The pot was split, dashed and dined. The court below held that the pot was a vote — that paying in was consent, that everyone who dined had voted, and that the one who has abstained for fifteen years consented by saying nothing.

The appeal is narrow and the page thinks it is right. Matriculating funds upward is not voting. A structure that takes money in, promotes from within, and reproduces itself in bodies that are not identical but identitarianly aligned has no step at which anybody is asked — and holding that the pot was the asking is precisely how it never adds one. Abstention is not consent. Fifteen years of it is not fifteen years of agreement; it is fifteen years of a question nobody put.

How the case comes up three keys, and they are all you hold

The grant — what is put before the bench

Nobody's mind changes when you move these. What moves is which of nine practised positions each judge is standing in when the question arrives — which is what a practice is, and why a bench is not an opinion poll.

Certiorari denied

Three or fewer. The judgment below stands, unreviewed and unreasoned, and the pot remains a vote in every county of a jurisdiction the size of England — not because a court held that it was, but because no court held anything. Seat the ninth chair and count again.

Denial is not disagreement and it is not a small thing. Nothing on this page is more consequential than that first number, and it is the number the fewest people ever look at.

The judgment eight of twelve circuits reverse

Reversed

The pot was not a vote. Paying in is not consent, dining is not ratification, and the fifteen-year abstention is an abstention — it says what it has always said, which is nothing, and nothing may not be read as yes. The holding below is vacated across all nine thousand square miles.

The court below stands. Put to this bench in leaf, or put to it as the pot itself, five of nine will hold that the money was the meaning — and they are not fools, they are reading a structure that has genuinely never had a step where anyone was asked, and concluding that the pot must therefore have been it. That is the error. It is an extremely easy one.

Equally divided · four to four

Eight sitting, four each way, no opinion, no precedent, and the judgment below stands by default rather than by reasoning. Look at which chair is empty. Both of the circuits on this board that end four to four end that way because the abstainer was not asked to sit, and a court that cannot break a tie without her is a court that was always deciding her case without her in it.

Why the first vote is the whole case the page being opinionated

A syndicate does not vote. It matriculates funds upward and promotes from within, and those are one motion seen from either end. Being cyclical, it reproduces — not a copy but a body identitarianly aligned with itself, mimicking the structure closely enough to be run the same way by the same hands. Less amoeba than germ: cohesive as one organism, factional in its parts, and the factions are hats rather than people. There is no love in it and no sex and no survival. It is a cycle that learned to make another of itself, and the reason it looks transparent the moment you see it is that it was never hiding — it simply has no step at which anyone is asked.

Which is why the rule of four is the real holding of this page and the merits are the footnote. Four votes is the step where somebody gets asked. Deny cert and the structure keeps running with a court sitting right on top of it, unreversed, having decided nothing — and that is not a failure of the system, it is the system working exactly as built. The one reliable way to lose In re the Pot is to leave the ninth chair empty, and the ninth chair has been empty for fifteen years.
