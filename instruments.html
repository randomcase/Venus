The instruments

every figure, and how wrong it might be

Boards across this yard print 574 g/cm², 4.94 minutes, 2.05 degrees, 8.71 m/s². Until this layer existed, nothing recorded where any of them came from. A figure with no provenance is a rumour with a decimal point — so each of these declares its method, its uncertainty, what it is used for, and what would make it wrong.

the check nobody runs

The generator refuses an entry whose quoted precision is not justified by its own error bar. The rule is the ordinary one: the last digit you write should be the first digit you are unsure about.

So ±0.5 justifies one decimal place and 146.1 is fine. ±25 justifies none, and 574 is fine because the units digit is the uncertain one. But 150 ± 50 is a third of the value — that supports one significant figure, not three, and writing it as 150 claims a confidence the measurement does not have.

This is not a wrong-number check. It is an over-confident-number check, and that is the harder mistake to see because it looks exactly like rigour.

A wide bar only matters when the argument is quantitative, and saying which is which is most of what this page is for.

Gravity at the deck

derived8.71 m/s2± 0.02
how it was arrived at

Surface gravity from GM and the mean radius, then reduced by the inverse square to 55 km. Both inputs are known to far better than the figure needs, so the error here is the altitude, not the mass.

what it is used for

Everything about the weight-versus-inertia argument: settling velocity, sediment carriage, and whether a person can work.

what would make it wrong

The deck is not held at 55 km. Every tenth of a kilometre moves this in the fourth decimal, so drift in station-keeping is a bigger error source than the constants.

Atmospheric shielding above 55 km

derived570 g/cm2± 25
how it was arrived at

Integrated column mass above 55 km from a standard Venus atmosphere model. The uncertainty is the model, not the integration. Rounded: rounded from 574: a ±25 bar owns the tens digit.

what it is used for

The claim that radiation is not the problem on the deck.

what would make it wrong

The atmosphere model is wrong above 55 km, which it may be — the density profile there is less well constrained than lower down, and a systematic error would move all of it one way.

Light delay at arrival

computed4.94 minutes± 0.02
how it was arrived at

Range between the two planets at the arrival geometry, divided by c. Recomputed per frame in game.html against a Kepler solve rather than taken from a table.

what it is used for

The relay, the corps autonomy window, and the whole argument that nothing on the deck can be run in real time from Earth.

what would make it wrong

The arrival geometry is not the one assumed. This is a figure for one moment in one trajectory, not a constant, and quoting it as though it were a property of Venus is the mistake to watch for.

Spica, ecliptic latitude

computed2.05 degrees± 0.01
how it was arrived at

beta = asin(sin d cos e - cos d sin e sin a), from the J2000 catalogue position and the obliquity. Computed in the generator rather than looked up.

what it is used for

Why Venus and Spica keep meeting, on the notebook cover.

what would make it wrong

Nothing plausible. This is arithmetic on published constants and the only way it moves is if the catalogue position is wrong, which it is not.

Minimum-energy transfer, Earth to Venus

computed146.1 days± 0.5
how it was arrived at

Half the period of the transfer ellipse, from vis-viva and Kepler. Reproduces the published 146 d, which is the check that the rest of the transit curve is worth reading.

what it is used for

The whole transit argument, and the six-months-is-a-choice section.

what would make it wrong

The orbits are treated as circular and coplanar. They are neither, and a real mission planner would get a different number by a day or two depending on the launch window.

Bond albedo of Venus

measured0.77 fraction± 0.02
how it was arrived at

Spacecraft radiometry, integrated over the sphere and the spectrum.

what it is used for

The inversion: that Venus absorbs less sunlight than Earth. This single number is what carries that finding.

what would make it wrong

It is nearer 0.75 or 0.80, which is within argument. At 0.70 the inversion narrows sharply, and the finding is more sensitive to this figure than to anything else in it.

Greenhouse warming at the Venus surface

derived510 K± 8
how it was arrived at

Surface temperature minus the effective temperature computed from absorbed flux. The uncertainty is inherited almost entirely from the albedo.

what it is used for

The factor of fifteen against Earth, which is the divergence.

what would make it wrong

The albedo is wrong. Everything else in the chain is arithmetic.

Deuterium to hydrogen, Venus against Earth

measured150 times± 50
how it was arrived at

Mass spectrometry from atmospheric probes. The published range is wide and the figure is usually quoted as an order of magnitude.

what it is used for

The claim that Venus had an ocean and lost it.

what would make it wrong

Nothing that changes the conclusion. Anything from 100 to 200 carries the same argument, which is why the wide bar does not matter here and would matter enormously if the argument were quantitative rather than directional.

Rossby deformation radius on Venus

derived2400 km± 400
how it was arrived at

Standard deformation radius from the scale height, buoyancy frequency and the Coriolis parameter at mid-latitude. The buoyancy term is the loose one. Rounded: rounded from 2363: a ±400 bar owns the hundreds.

what it is used for

You can dig a dust bowl, you cannot dig a hurricane.

what would make it wrong

The buoyancy frequency is off by a factor, which would move this proportionally. The confirmation is that the polar vortices are observed at about 2,000 km, which was not arranged and is the reason to believe the calculation.

Venus solar day

computed116.75 Earth days± 0.01
how it was arrived at

From the sidereal rotation and the orbital period, both measured to many figures.

what it is used for

The 58-day night, which sets the entire lighting budget.

what would make it wrong

Nothing. This is among the best-known numbers in the document.

Hall of Mirrors, length

measured73 metres± 1
how it was arrived at

Published dimension, widely repeated and consistent across sources.

what it is used for

The gallery on two castle plans, and the 357-over-17 division that the generator refuses to let drift.

what would make it wrong

Sources disagree by a metre or so about where the room is measured from. It does not matter for anything here.

Pitch of the hall stair

computed23 degrees± 0.1
how it was arrived at

atan(rise / going) from the declared 153 mm by 360 mm.

what it is used for

Whether the stair reads as processional or as a ladder, and the comparison against a 42-degree service stair.

what would make it wrong

Nothing — it is arithmetic on two numbers I chose. This is a DESIGN figure rather than a measurement, and the layer marks it as such so nobody mistakes it for a fact about the world.
