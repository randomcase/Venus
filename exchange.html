Johannes Junius

the burgomaster’s house · Bamberg · 1628 · 4,200 deck hours

stated cause: witchcraft, confessed under torture

He was burgomaster of Bamberg — wealthy, and holding office in a city running mass trials. His letter to his daughter survives, written from the cell: the confession was false and he said so in writing while he still could.

The men in the warlock archive are not poor. When a man was taken it was frequently office and estate.

Dietrich Flade

a senior judge’s estate · Trier · 1589 · 6,800 deck hours

stated cause: witchcraft, after he grew sceptical of the trials

He was a judge at Trier who began doubting the evidence in front of him. He was tried and executed in 1589.

Doubt was survivable only in silence. The board records this one because it is what happens to the sceptic.

Sarah Osborne

a disputed widow’s plot · Salem Village · 1692 · 900 deck hours

stated cause: witchcraft, among the first three accused

A widow who remarried an indentured servant and fought her own sons in court over the estate. She was bedridden when arrested and died in the Boston jail on 10 May 1692, before any trial.

She was never tried. The property fight preceded the accusation and outlived her.

Tituba

her own body, to cover the fees · Salem Village · 1693 · 380 deck hours

stated cause: none — she was never convicted of anything

Enslaved, roughly 30 to 40, no civil rights. She confessed and survived. Held fourteen months, and because prisoners were charged for their own imprisonment, she was SOLD to cover her jail fees.

THE CHARGE COLLAPSED AND THE ACCOUNTING DID NOT. This is the one to read twice: the supernatural case went nowhere and the invoice was still enforceable.

Giles Corey DEFEATED THE SEIZURE

a prosperous farm · Salem Farms · 1692 · 3,100 deck hours

stated cause: witchcraft — but he would not plead

Refusing to plead meant no trial, no conviction, and therefore no forfeiture. He was pressed under stones for two days and never entered a plea. His sons inherited.

THE ONLY DEFEAT OF THE MECHANISM IN THIS ARCHIVE. He beat the seizure and it cost him everything else, which is why it is here: the board should not read as though this were inevitable.

the enslaved of the British Caribbean

the compensation, paid to owners · Britain · 1837 · 2,000,000 deck hours

stated cause: the abolition of slavery

The Slave Compensation Act 1837 paid roughly £20 million — near 40 per cent of annual Treasury spending — to slave OWNERS, for loss of property. The people who had been enslaved received nothing. It was financed by borrowing only fully repaid in 2015.

Restitution is the wrong word for what happened. Compensation occurred and it went the other way, and everybody alive in Britain before 2015 helped service the loan.

Haiti

the price of its own independence · Port-au-Prince · 1825 · 1,500,000 deck hours

stated cause: recognition of the republic

France required 150 million francs in exchange for recognising Haitian independence, under the guns of a fleet. Haiti serviced that debt into the twentieth century.

The largest single entry on this board, and it was charged to the people who had freed themselves, for having done it.

what this board refuses

An entry with no named party. A seizure with nobody named is exactly what makes a seizure invisible, and the generator will not write one. The from whom column is what makes the taken column mean anything.
And an entry with no stated cause — because the cause is what was claimed, and it belongs on the record beside what was true. Every row here carries both, and the gap between them is the whole subject.
Nothing here is invented. Junius, Flade, Osborne, Tituba, Corey, the compensation of 1837, Haiti in 1825. An invented seizure would make this a fantasy about a system rather than a record of one, and the names being checkable is the entire value of the board.
And Giles Corey is in here because he won. He refused to plead, so there was no conviction and therefore no forfeiture, and his sons inherited. It is the only defeat of the mechanism in this archive and it cost him two days under stones — but the board should not read as though any of this were inevitable, and it is not.
