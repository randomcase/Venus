never charged at all
13

Physicians, friars, founders, a court calendar official and one canonised saint. They are here because somebody filed them here.

The category is not a description of these people. It is a description of who was doing the filing. Ibn Sina wrote a medical text European universities taught for six hundred years. Al-Razi was the first to separate smallpox from measles clinically. Albertus Magnus is a saint. Bodhidharma founded the tradition that becomes Zen. None of them stood in a dock, and all of them are in a warlock archive.

This is the shape the archetype board already found from the other side: the power was attributed by somebody, and the attribution is the whole event.

Rev. George Burroughs

triedwest

Salem, Massachusetts · 1692 · hanged

A minister, and a former minister of Salem Village itself.

Recited the Lord’s Prayer perfectly on the gallows, which was held to be impossible for a witch. He was hanged anyway.

Giles Corey

triedwest

Salem Farms · 1692 · pressed to death

A prosperous landowner in his eighties.

Refused to plead, so there was no conviction and no forfeiture. Two days under stones and his sons inherited. The only defeat of the mechanism in this archive.

Johannes Junius

triedwest

Bamberg · 1628 · burned

Burgomaster of Bamberg.

His letter to his daughter survives, written from the cell: the confession was false and extracted, and he said so in writing while he still could.

Dietrich Flade

triedwest

Trier · 1589 · executed

A senior judge, and wealthy.

He grew sceptical of the evidence in front of him and was tried himself. Doubt was survivable only in silence.

Urbain Grandier

triedwest

Loudun · 1634 · burned

A priest with powerful political enemies, including Richelieu.

Convicted on the testimony of possessed nuns. The trial is one of the best-documented in Europe and reads throughout as a factional settling.

John Fian

triedwest

North Berwick · 1591 · executed

A schoolmaster.

Accused of raising the storm that struck James VI’s ship. The king attended the examinations personally and later wrote a book about demonology.

Major Thomas Weir

triedwest

Edinburgh · 1670 · strangled and burned

A respected Covenanter officer, seventy years old.

He confessed unprompted to crimes nobody had accused him of. The one case here where the confession preceded the accusation.

François Makandal

triedwest

Saint-Domingue · 1758 · burned

An enslaved man, a maroon leader, and by every account a genuinely skilled herbalist.

Organised a poisoning campaign against planters. The charge was sorcery; the activity was insurrection, and the colonial court had reason to prefer the first word.

Giordano Bruno

triedwest

Rome · 1600 · burned

A Dominican friar, philosopher and cosmologist.

Held that the stars were suns with worlds of their own. The charges were theological rather than sorcerous, and he is here because the archive filed him here — which is itself the point.

scholar never tried for it, and doing something else entirely · 8

Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

scholarelsewhere

Persia · 1037 · died of illness, at court

A physician and philosopher.

Wrote the Canon of Medicine, which was a standard text in European universities for roughly six hundred years. Filing him under warlock is a Western taxonomy applied to a man who had a different job.

Al-Razi (Rhazes)

scholarelsewhere

Rayy and Baghdad · 925 · died blind, in poverty

A physician and chemist.

First clear clinical distinction between smallpox and measles, and an early sceptic of alchemy as transmutation while doing the chemistry anyway.

Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber)

scholarelsewhere

Kufa · 815 · unknown

A chemist, or a corpus of chemists writing under one name.

The word alchemy carries an Arabic article on the front of it because of this tradition. Distillation, crystallisation and classification of substances are all in the corpus.

Roger Bacon

scholarwest

Oxford and Paris · 1292 · died a friar

A Franciscan friar and natural philosopher.

Argued for experiment and mathematics against argument from authority. Later legend made him a magician; he was a monk with a lens.

Albertus Magnus

scholarwest

Cologne · 1280 · died a bishop, later canonised

A Dominican friar, Aquinas’s teacher.

Catalogued plants and minerals systematically. He is a SAINT, and he is in a warlock archive, and both of those are true at once.

John Dee

scholarwest

Mortlake · 1609 · died poor

Mathematician, navigator, and Elizabeth I’s astrologer.

Assembled the largest library in England and advised on navigation for the northern voyages. Also spent years attempting to speak with angels through Edward Kelley, and both halves are him.

Paracelsus

scholarwest

Basel and Salzburg · 1541 · died at forty-seven

A physician.

"The dose makes the poison" is his, and it is the founding sentence of toxicology. He also burned Avicenna’s books in public, which tells you something about the temperament.

Ge Hong

scholarelsewhere

Jin China · 343 · died in retirement

A Daoist writer and physician.

The Baopuzi contains the earliest known description of smallpox symptoms and practical chemistry alongside the immortality material. Both are in the same book by the same person.

Bodhidharma

teacherelsewhere

China, from South India · 536 · tradition disputes it

The traditional founder of Chan Buddhism.

Chan becomes Zen. He is a religious founder for hundreds of millions of people and appears here because a Western category was laid over him from outside.

Padmasambhava

teacherelsewhere

Tibet · 800 · tradition says he did not die

The figure credited with establishing Buddhism in Tibet.

Called Guru Rinpoche by millions of living practitioners. The "sorcery" is a tradition of tantric practice described by people outside it.

Zhang Daoling

teacherelsewhere

Sichuan · 156 · tradition says he ascended

Founder of the Way of the Celestial Masters.

The first organised Daoist church. A religious institution, filed under sorcery by a taxonomy that had no other box for it.

Milarepa

teacherelsewhere

Tibet · 1135 · poisoned, by tradition

Tibet’s most celebrated poet and yogi.

His life story opens with him using sorcery for revenge and spends the rest of itself on the consequences. The tradition itself treats the magic as the sin.

Abe no Seimei

teacherelsewhere

Heian Japan · 1005 · died at eighty-four, honoured

An onmyōji — a court diviner and calendar official.

A civil servant. Onmyōdō was a government bureau handling the calendar, timekeeping and divination, and he ran it well enough to be remembered for a thousand years.
