02 · the number

Twenty-one million, and not one more

The cap is not a marketing figure carried over from somewhere else. It is the constraint the rest of the design was built to satisfy, and everything downstream had to clear it.

Cap21,000,000

Whole units. Divisible for accounting, never extended.

Combination floor21,000,000

The design rule: a coin must address at least this many distinct hulls, so no two are forced to collide.

Actually achieved1.536 × 10¹²

73,142 times the floor, counting only the discrete slots.

Why the floor matters. If the number of buildable hulls were smaller than the number of coins, coins would have to share hulls, and a hull would stop being a thing a coin could stand for. The forge clears it by six orders of magnitude before the continuous axes — the length, beam, freeboard, sheer and rig sliders — are counted at all. With those, the space is around 2 × 10²⁵.

03 · what stands behind it

A costed fleet, not a promise

Every hull in the forge is priced from the geometry that was actually drawn: cubic metres of oak, metres of seam to caulk, square metres of cloth, kilos of ironwork, and shipwright hours. Change a strake count and the timber volume changes, so the price changes, because there is more boat.

04 · issuance

One address in, one hull out

A public address is read as a number and seeded into the forge. The same address always yields the same ship — that determinism is the only property that makes a build worth claiming.

Step oneRead

The address is hashed locally with FNV-1a into a seed. It is never transmitted, and it is never signed with.

Step twoDraw

The seed picks hull, element, method, twelve slots, palette, finish, fastening and every proportion. Then the ship is drawn.

Step threeCount

The bill of materials falls out of the drawing — strakes, frames, fastenings, seams — and the cost falls out of the bill.

Build the ten. One address also lays down a whole fleet: ten hulls, one per tradition, each seeded from address:index:hull so they are ten different ships rather than ten paint jobs on one. The fleet is berthed locally and costed at the current yard rates.

Nothing leaves the file. There is no network call in the forge. The address sits in an input, is read, and is used to pick options. Closing the tab ends it.

06 · what this is not

Read this one twice

The five slides before this describe a unit of account inside a set of local HTML files. This slide describes everything it isn't, and none of it is hedging — each line is a fact about the code.

There is no token. No chain, no contract, no deployment, no address that holds anything. 0x48455a45 is the ASCII for “HEZE” printed on a docket, not a function selector on any network.
Nothing here can be bought or sold. These files have no transfer, no order book, no custody and no counterparty. There is nothing to acquire, so nothing on these slides is an offer, a solicitation or a sale.
The dollar figures are a costing, not a valuation. $20,387,410 is what ten drawn hulls would cost to build at rates you can edit. Dividing it by 21,000,000 gives a ratio between two numbers in this project. It is not a price, it does not imply one, and no one is obliged to honour it.
Nothing is signed and nothing is sent. The address field is read locally to seed a drawing. There is no network call, no wallet connection, no signature request, and no key ever touches these files.
This is not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything, and nobody involved is a licensed adviser.

What it is: a design document for the unit a fictional shipyard quotes in, sitting alongside two files that draw ships and count their fastenings. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
