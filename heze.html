02 · the number

Twenty-one million, and not one more

The cap is not a marketing figure carried over from somewhere else. It is the constraint the rest of the design was built to satisfy, and everything downstream had to clear it.

Cap21,000,000

Whole units. Divisible for accounting, never extended.

Combination floor21,000,000

The design rule: a coin must address at least this many distinct hulls, so no two are forced to collide.

Actually achieved1.536 × 10¹²

73,142 times the floor, counting only the discrete slots.

Why the floor matters. If the number of buildable hulls were smaller than the number of coins, coins would have to share hulls, and a hull would stop being a thing a coin could stand for. The forge clears it by six orders of magnitude before the continuous axes — the length, beam, freeboard, sheer and rig sliders — are counted at all. With those, the space is around 2 × 10²⁵.

04 · issuance

One address in, one hull out

A public address is read as a number and seeded into the forge. The same address always yields the same ship — that determinism is the only property that makes a build worth claiming.

Step oneRead

The address is hashed locally with FNV-1a into a seed. It is never transmitted, and it is never signed with.

Step twoDraw

The seed picks hull, element, method, twelve slots, palette, finish, fastening and every proportion. Then the ship is drawn.

Step threeCount

The bill of materials falls out of the drawing — strakes, frames, fastenings, seams — and the cost falls out of the bill.

Build the ten. One address also lays down a whole fleet: ten hulls, one per tradition, each seeded from address:index:hull so they are ten different ships rather than ten paint jobs on one. The fleet is berthed locally and costed at the current yard rates.

Nothing leaves the file. There is no network call in the forge. The address sits in an input, is read, and is used to pick options. Closing the tab ends it.

06 · lesson one, fungibility

Any one HEZE is every HEZE

Fungibility means two units of the same thing are interchangeable — holding one is exactly as good as holding any other, with nothing about its past attached to it. It is one of two properties this whole design was built to keep, and the easiest way to see it is to compare HEZE against something in these same files that is deliberately not fungible.

HEZE, door 1 vs door 200No difference

A unit issued at the first door and a unit issued at the two-hundredth are the same unit. Neither deck may ask which door a HEZE came from before accepting it.

A hull from the forgeAlways different

Two ships of the same class, ordered from two different addresses, are drawn from two different seeds — different strakes, different cost, a different bill of materials. That is the opposite of fungible: identity is the whole point.

The rule, in one lineNo history

A fungible unit carries no record of where it has been that changes what it is worth. The moment one HEZE became worth more than another because of its past, it would stop being a unit of account and start being a collectible.

Why it has to be this way. A price list only works if every buyer is quoting against the same thing. The order desk two slides back prices a Destroyer at 12,400 HEZE for anyone at any door — the moment two doors' HEZE stopped being interchangeable, the price list would need one column per door instead of one column, period.

07 · lesson two, decentralization

Decentralized where it is priced, not where it is kept honest

Decentralization is not one property a design either has or lacks — it is a choice about which specific part of a system is allowed to act on its own, and which part exists to reconcile what the independent parts did. HEZE's docket makes that choice explicitly rather than picking a slogan.

Decentralized200 doors

Each door issues against its own local share of a tranche, on its own schedule, with no door needing another door's permission to move.

Centralized21 syndicates

Two of three custodians per syndicate sign before anything is real, and every door's events are reconciled into one chain the syndicate keeps.

Why bothNeither alone is enough

200 doors with no reconciliation would drift apart with no way to catch a contradiction. One central authority with no independent doors would only ever be checking itself.

What this is not. It is not a claim that nobody is in charge — two of three custodians signing is a real, named authority, on purpose. It is a claim about where that authority sits: at the reconciliation, not at any one door, and never in one signature alone.

08 · lesson three, automation

Nothing runs that a person did not run first

Real crypto systems automate constantly — bots, smart contracts, scheduled jobs — and a large share of real losses trace back to code that ran unattended before anyone had actually checked the sequence by hand. HEZE's own terminal draws the line on purpose: record, run, then automate, always in that order.

Away timeCaught up, not guessed

Up to eight hours away is replayed one simulated day at a time — the same proposals, the same rolls, the same result you would have gotten watching it happen, never a shortcut.

Auto-signOpt in, per desk

Custodians only sign on their own once told to, at the same odds a person signing by hand would face — automation changes who is at the keyboard, not the rules being followed.

Record → run → automateIn that order

A command sequence is typed and watched until it is right, replayed once to confirm it, and only then set on an interval. The macro is a transcript of a person, not a program nobody read.

The real-world version of this mistake. A contract that moves funds on a trigger nobody tested against every input, or a bot that was live before its first run was ever watched — both are automation that skipped the middle step. The terminal's rule exists because that step is the only one that catches a mistake before it repeats forever.

09 · lesson four, syndication

Carrying a chain to another chain, and proving it later

Bridging one ledger's history into another's is one of the oldest sources of real losses in crypto — a forged root, waved through because nobody on the receiving side could actually check it. HEZE's syndication is the same idea, built with the check included rather than assumed.

Every six monthsThe interval

Whatever a syndicate's doors signed since the last carry crosses at the interval, whether or not anything was ready — the checkpoint is taken either way, so a quiet interval is provable too.

A checkpoint, chainedSHA-256 over the last

Each syndication hashes the events it carried together with the previous checkpoint's own hash, so breaking one checkpoint without breaking every one after it is not possible.

The far side, at scaleReconciles, does not answer

The other world's chain arrives with orders of magnitude more volume than these doors did and reconciles without comment — not contempt, just scale, and the same scale is what keeps any one door from being singled out.

The check that actually matters. A checkpoint you cannot recompute is a claim, not a proof. Verifying a crossing means holding both chains and recomputing the source's own Merkle root at the exact sequence the checkpoint names — that is the one thing a forged root cannot survive, and it is the templated attack this design was tested against on purpose, not the one left out of the test suite.

10 · what this is not

Read this one twice

The nine slides before this describe a unit of account inside a set of local HTML files, and four real lessons taught through it. This slide describes everything it isn't, and none of it is hedging — each line is a fact about the code.

There is no token. No chain, no contract, no deployment, no address that holds anything. 0x48455a45 is the ASCII for “HEZE” printed on a docket, not a function selector on any network.
Nothing here can be bought or sold. These files have no transfer, no order book, no custody and no counterparty. There is nothing to acquire, so nothing on these slides is an offer, a solicitation or a sale.
The dollar figures are a costing, not a valuation. $20,387,410 is what ten drawn hulls would cost to build at rates you can edit. Dividing it by 21,000,000 gives a ratio between two numbers in this project. It is not a price, it does not imply one, and no one is obliged to honour it.
Nothing is signed and nothing is sent. The address field is read locally to seed a drawing. There is no network call, no wallet connection, no signature request, and no key ever touches these files.
This is not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything, and nobody involved is a licensed adviser.

What it is: a design document for the unit a fictional shipyard quotes in, sitting alongside two files that draw ships and count their fastenings. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
