what happened after the war; where the idea went next; what became of every nation that defended Saigon and withdrew

Full stop on Saigon. The rooms end there. This page begins there: the Americans left, every nation that had contributed to the funded war effort in the defence of Saigon withdrew with them, and then communism — the thing the whole manner of resistance had been built to contain — went on, both in Vietnam and in the world, until it did not.

Vietnam, after

1975-05Re-education: about 200,000–300,000 former ARVN officers, officials and professionals sent to camps; some for months, some for seventeen years.
1975-12Laos: the monarchy abolished; the Pathet Lao take the state without a battle. The Hmong who fought for the CIA are hunted for a decade.
1976-07Reunification: the Socialist Republic of Vietnam; Saigon renamed; collectivisation in the South begins and fails.
1975-04 to 1979-01Cambodia: the Khmer Rouge empty the cities on the first day and kill 1.5–2 million of 8 million in four years.
1978-12Vietnam invades Cambodia and ends the Khmer Rouge in two weeks; the world, including the United States and China, keeps recognising the Khmer Rouge at the UN until 1991.
1979-02China invades Vietnam to punish it for Cambodia; a month, tens of thousands dead on each side; two communist states at war, which the domino theory had not allowed for.
1975–1995The boat people: about 800,000 reach other shores; perhaps 200,000–400,000 do not. The Orderly Departure Program from 1979; the refugees resettle in the United States, Australia, Canada, France.
1986-12Doi Moi, renovation: the party keeps the state and gives up the economy. Vietnam becomes a market with a politburo.
1994-02The US embargo lifted; 1995-07, diplomatic relations; 2000, Clinton in Hanoi; 2016, the arms embargo lifted; 2023, a comprehensive strategic partnership. Vietnam is now, by treaty, a partner of the nation it defeated, against the neighbour that armed it.
stillThe party rules. The camps are closed. The Wall in Washington has 58,000 names and Vietnam has no wall, and the toll on its side is still a range, not a number.

The spread, and the retreat

Where communism went next

1974-09Ethiopia: the Derg.
1975-11Angola: the MPLA, with Cuban troops; a proxy war for sixteen years.
1975-06Mozambique: FRELIMO.
1978-04Afghanistan: the Saur coup; the Soviet invasion in December 1979; the American answer through the mujahideen, the war after this war.
1979-07Nicaragua: the Sandinistas; the contras; the Boland amendments, written by the Congress that had learned from Vietnam; Iran–Contra, the executive learning nothing.
1979-03Grenada: the New Jewel Movement; the American invasion of October 1983, the first ground war after Vietnam, four days long, chosen for the length.
1980-08Poland: Solidarity — the first crack from inside.
1989-06Tiananmen; and the same month Poland votes; November, the Wall.
1991-12The Soviet Union dissolves. Of the twenty-five, five remain: China, Vietnam, Laos, Cuba, North Korea — three of them the parties to this war.
nowThe five are still five. Two of them, China and Vietnam, are the world’s factory and its fastest-growing partner respectively, and each other’s adversary. The idea outlived its containment by being, in the end, less contained than adapted.

Neighbours are not harbours, unless

Neighbours are not harbours. Every nation on the contributors’ list was a neighbour of the war before it was a party to it, and every one that took refugees afterward became a harbour by choice rather than by geography. The exception is the rule’s proof: Cambodia and Laos, the nearest neighbours, could be neither, and were taken. In the ledger this repository keeps, that is the difference between a syndicate that can audit its neighbour and one that can only be carried across by it.
