Gerald Ford

Project 44 · presidential suite · from templates-president/ford.json · index · repo randomcase/gerald-ford

Republican · 1974–1977 · the American standard: the president who presided over its ending

Role. Presided over the Fall of Saigon. Not one of the four who managed the war; the one who managed its ending: asked Congress for the aid Nixon had promised in private, was refused, and ordered the evacuation.

Decisions, one line item each

1974-08-09Sworn in. Writes to Thieu the next day that the commitments of his predecessor still stand; they do not, because Congress holds them.
1975-01-06Phuoc Long province falls, the first whole province lost since 1973. No US response; Hanoi reads it as the test it was.
1975-01-28Asks Congress for $300 million more for Saigon; hearings, no vote.
1975-03-25Sends Weyand to Saigon to assess. Weyand reports on 4 April: it can hold with $722 million and air support; Ford knows the second is illegal and the first is unlikely.
1975-04-03Operation Babylift ordered; the first C-5A crashes the next day, 138 dead.
1975-04-10Address to Congress asking for the $722 million; several members walk out.
1975-04-23Tulane: the war is “finished as far as America is concerned”. The line is applauded; Kissinger was not told it was in the speech.
1975-04-28Approves Frequent Wind in principle; Ambassador Martin still resists.
1975-04-29Orders the evacuation at 1048 Saigon time after the rocket attack on Tan Son Nhut kills the last two Marines.
1975-04-30Saigon falls at 1130 local. Ford’s statement: the evacuation is complete; it “closes a chapter”.
1975-05-12The Mayaguez, seized by the Khmer Rouge: 41 Americans die retaking a ship whose crew had already been released. The last names on the Wall.

The lead-up, a day at a time

1975-03-10Ban Me Thuot attacked before dawn by three PAVN divisions; falls in two days.
1975-03-11Thieu tells his generals the Highlands may have to be given up; Ban Me Thuot lost.
1975-03-13Thieu decides at Cam Ranh to withdraw from Pleiku and Kontum down Route 7B, an abandoned road.
1975-03-14The order given; General Phu leaves by helicopter before his troops.
1975-03-16The Route 7B column of 200,000 soldiers and civilians is caught at the Ba River. The convoy of tears.
1975-03-18Quang Tri abandoned. In Washington, Ford’s aid request is still in committee.
1975-03-19Thieu reverses the order to hold Hue: hold it. Then reverses again.
1975-03-21Route 1 cut south of Hue; the city is isolated.
1975-03-24Tam Ky and Quang Ngai fall; the coast is a line of retreats.
1975-03-25Hue falls. Ford sends Weyand.
1975-03-26Chu Lai evacuated by sea.
1975-03-27Weyand arrives in Saigon; the Da Nang airlift begins with 727s landing under fire.
1975-03-28Panic at Da Nang; the last World Airways flight leaves with soldiers on the wheel wells.
1975-03-29Da Nang falls. A hundred thousand ARVN gone as an army in a week. The gauge now reads about 24 provinces of 44.
1975-03-31Hanoi’s Politburo moves the target from 1976 to April 1975: the Ho Chi Minh Campaign.
1975-04-01Qui Nhon and Nha Trang fall; Thieu’s cabinet resigns.
1975-04-02Tuy Hoa; Weyand’s draft report says the situation is critical and the odds long.
1975-04-03Cam Ranh; Ford orders Babylift; refugee ships pulling into Vung Tau with the dead on deck.
1975-04-04The C-5A crashes on take-off from Tan Son Nhut, 138 dead, 78 of them children. Weyand reports to Ford at Palm Springs.
1975-04-08A South Vietnamese pilot bombs the presidential palace and flies north.
1975-04-09Xuan Loc: the PAVN 4th Corps attacks; the 18th Division holds. The one stand.
1975-04-10Ford’s address to Congress; the request is not taken up.
1975-04-12Operation Eagle Pull: the US embassy in Phnom Penh evacuated by helicopter. Sirik Matak declines the seat: he will die where he is, and does.
1975-04-14Ford reads a State Department cable predicting Saigon can hold a month; the CIA says less.
1975-04-17Phnom Penh falls to the Khmer Rouge. The dominoes fall in the wrong order.
1975-04-18Ford orders the evacuation of all non-essential Americans; Martin slows it, fearing panic.
1975-04-20Xuan Loc abandoned after twelve days; the road to Saigon open. Martin tells Thieu it is time to go.
1975-04-21Thieu resigns on television, blaming the Americans, in a speech of ninety minutes. Xuan Loc falls.
1975-04-23Ford at Tulane: finished. The Senate Armed Services Committee stops hearing the aid request.
1975-04-25Thieu flies to Taiwan on a CIA aircraft with what he can carry.
1975-04-26The PAVN begins the final assault from five directions; 130,000 troops against the city.
1975-04-27Rockets hit Saigon for the first time since 1973; Bien Hoa air base under fire.
1975-04-28Duong Van Minh — “Big Minh”, who overthrew Diem in 1963 — sworn in as president to negotiate; Tan Son Nhut bombed by captured A-37s flown by defectors.
1975-04-29Rockets at 0358 kill Corporal McMahon and Lance Corporal Judge at the DAO gate. Fixed-wing evacuation ends; Ford orders Frequent Wind at 1048; “White Christmas” on the radio; 81 helicopters, 19 hours.
1975-04-30Martin leaves the roof at 0458; the last eleven Marines at 0753. T-54 tank 843 through the palace gate at 1130; Minh surrenders; the city is Ho Chi Minh City by the afternoon. Ford’s statement, 1330 Washington time.
1975-05-01Ford asks Congress for $507 million for refugees; it passes on 21 May. The lead-up is over and the after begins in the section below.

Lives, after

Where fifty days after the fall cannot be had one line at a time, the lives can: the books, the art, the films, and the diagnoses. It was a lifelong conflict for a lot of men, and this is the record of that.
