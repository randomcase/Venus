Three ways to say the thesis

ToneThesis
academicWhile the 1973 Paris Peace Accords functionally concluded conventional military operations, the legacy of the Vietnam War persists through structural public policy battles over chemical exposure, technological innovations in post-conflict land reclamation, and the institutional reintegration of prisoners of war.
narrativeThe final echoes of the Vietnam War were not silenced by a signed peace treaty, but live on in the quiet endurance of prisoners returning home, the multi-generational fight against a toxic inheritance, and the high-tech battle to strip hidden explosives from a scarred earth.
argumentativeThe conventional timeline of the Vietnam War is fundamentally flawed; the true conclusion of the conflict spans decades of post-1973 activism, driven by veterans demanding medical justice for dioxin exposure and civilian communities using modern technology to reclaim their land from unexploded ordnance.

Operation Homecoming, 1973

1973-02-12First C-141 Starlifter out of Gia Lam, Hanoi: 116 prisoners. The aircraft were called the Freedom Birds; the cabin went silent until the wheels left the ground, and then it did not.
1973-02 to 1973-04591 prisoners repatriated in all — from Hanoi, from the Viet Cong in the South, from the Pathet Lao — in 54 flights; most were aircrew shot down over the North, some held nearly eight years.
1973-03-17Travis Air Force Base: Lt. Cdr. Robert L. Stirm meets his family on the tarmac. Sal Veder's photograph, Burst of Joy, wins the Pulitzer; Stirm had received his wife's letter ending the marriage three days before.
1973-03-29The last prisoners out on the day the last troops leave. The public celebrates the 591; 2,646 are then listed missing, and the MIA question becomes the war's longest argument.
the tap codeA 5×5 grid, the Polybius square with K folded into C: row taps, pause, column taps. B is tap, tap-tap; T is four, four. GN for good night through the walls every evening, GBU for God bless you. When the guards listened at the walls the prisoners swept, coughed, thumped a thigh, or blinked the same grid. Stockdale (In Love and War, 1984) describes the code and the rope torture it survived.

Agent Orange, the legal battle

1970s–1980sThe Veterans Administration denies claims: no proven link between dioxin and the veterans' cancers, it says, and the burden of proof is on the sick.
1984-05-07The class action against the manufacturers — Dow, Monsanto and five others — settles out of court for $180 million on the morning the trial was to begin. Payouts are small; long-term care is not covered.
1990Admiral Elmo Zumwalt, who as commander of the brown-water navy had ordered the spraying along the rivers, testifies and writes the report that lists the diseases; his son Elmo III, who served on those rivers, had died of lymphoma in 1988.
1991-02-06The Agent Orange Act: the VA must presume certain diseases — Hodgkin's, non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, soft-tissue sarcoma, and a list that grows to fourteen — are service-connected for anyone who set foot in Vietnam. The burden of proof moves.
2019-06-25The Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act: the sailors offshore, excluded for twenty-eight years, are included.
2022-08-10The PACT Act: toxic-exposure presumptions extended to the burn-pit generation, on the precedent the Vietnam veterans fought for. The Vietnamese exposed, some three million by Hanoi's count, and their children, have no act.

The ordnance, and the technology clearing it

the groundAbout 30% of cluster submunitions failed; some 80 million in Laos, most of Quang Tri province in Vietnam contaminated; about 20,000 Lao and 100,000 Vietnamese killed or injured since 1975.
by handFor forty years: a metal detector, a foot patrol, a technician kneeling. Still the last step.
from the airDrones with LiDAR map the ground under the canopy and find the craters; thermal cameras at dawn and dusk pick out metal that heats and cools faster than soil; magnetometers find the iron; ground-penetrating radar gives the depth before anyone digs.
the dataThe US Air Force's own bombing records — the THOR database, sortie by sortie with coordinates — are cross-referenced against the drone maps to rank the ground by probability before a team is sent.
the peopleMAG, HALO, PeaceTrees, UXO Lao: the technicians are local, and a large share are women from the villages, in the best-paid technical work the provinces have. Farmers photograph what the plough turns up and send the coordinates; children learn the bombies by song and puppet show; cleared land goes to coffee and pepper the same season.

How the country saw the men who came back

1973–1979Scapegoating and silence: a televised, unpopular war and the public's anger landed on the troops; men changed out of uniform in the airport; the film veteran was unstable; PTSD had no name until 1980.
1982-11-13The Wall. Grief given a place; the war separated from the warrior; the apology begins. Project 44 has the rest of the lives.
1991The Gulf War and the vow never to do it again to the ones coming home; the Agent Orange Act the same year: from complicit to survivors.
2000s–nowHonor flights, thanks for service, the PACT Act; a consensus of regret for 1973. The veterans are in their late seventies and eighties; about 600,000 of the 2.7 million who served in country are still alive.
