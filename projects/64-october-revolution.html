Phases

1917–1917
July Days

16–17 July: half a million in the streets demanding Soviet power, without the party's order; the government fires; hundreds dead; Lenin flees to Finland; Trotsky is jailed. The rising the Bolsheviks did not want, and learned from.

1917–1917
Kornilov

August: the commander-in-chief moves on Petrograd; Kerensky arms the Soviet to stop him; the Bolsheviks come out of jail with rifles the government gave them and a majority in the Soviet by September.

1917–1917
The night

24–26 October: the papers closed, the bridges raised and retaken, the buildings occupied, the Aurora, the palace at 2:10 a.m., the Congress. Fewer than a dozen dead in the capital; six on the government side at the palace by most counts.

1917–1918
The ratification, and its refusal

The Constituent Assembly elected in November: Bolsheviks 175 of 715 seats, SRs 370. It met once, on 5 January 1918, rejected the decrees, and was dissolved the next morning by the sailors on guard. Brest-Litovsk in March; the civil war, 1918–1922, about ten million dead.

Outcome

A party of perhaps 350,000 in a country of 150 million took the capital in a night with almost no one killed, and held it for seventy-four years. The revolution was the ratification; the seizure was the night before; the war was after. Every room in this index that contains an idea is downstream of this one.

Open questions

  • Whether it was a coup or a revolution: the night was a coup by any definition, the Congress that ratified it had a Bolshevik–Left SR majority, and the Assembly elected a month later did not. Both facts are in the room.
  • Whether Kerensky could have held: the garrison had gone over by the 21st; the question is whether September's decisions or July's made that so.
  • Whether the Assembly's dissolution on 6 January 1918, not the night of 25 October, is the date the idea stopped asking for consent.
