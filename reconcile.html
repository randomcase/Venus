The reconciliation

one unit, and who was left out of it

Six boards compute in six units and none of them share. Patrons prices in deck hours, the parliament counts oatcoins, the treasury mints five more, the atelier works in hectares and the aviary in keepers. Nothing was ever expressed in anything else, so nothing was ever added up.

217%of the crew’s year, once the farm work is entered

The patrons book funds 58,140 hours of boards. The aviary computes that feeding this crew from eggs alone takes 8 keepers, and the atelier computes 1.54 hectares of oats and the water to go with it — 18,209 hours between them. None of it appears anywhere in the book.

Add it and the yard is committed to 76,349 hours against a crew year of 35,160. Over by 41,189.

And the patrons book was already over before any of that. It commits 58,140 hours against a rested crew year of 35,160 — 165% on its own. That board reported itself at 99% because it measured against a crew that never rests. This file found that by converting it, which is what a reconciliation is for.

The book balanced because the farm work was never in it. That is not an accounting error. A ledger that balances by leaving a class out is the ordinary form of servitude, and this yard already has the entry for it: the compensation of 1837 paid owners and not the people who did the work, and the loan was serviced until 2015. Same shape, one board over.

the rest policy declared, because the first version assumed it away

The first version of this board had it wrong. It computed the crew year as eight hours a day, every day, 365 days — no rest day and no study. That is not a crew year, it is a number nobody can work, and the book only looked survivable because it was measured against a crew that never stops.

The generous year is roughly half the brutal one — 35,160 hours against 58,440. So being generous to the turnover does not make the arithmetic easier. It makes the overcommitment roughly twice as large, and it was always that large — the earlier figure was borrowing against rest nobody was going to get.
And study is a line, not a footnote. 120 hours a head a year for horticulture is 2,400 hours off the top. Learning is not production, and a district that is going to know something has to be given the time to learn it or it will not know it.

the base and why it is the base

The deck hour. One hour actually worked. It is the base for one reason: it is the only unit in this yard that cannot be printed. Everything else can be issued, minted, declared or grown. An hour has to be worked by somebody, so a book kept in hours cannot be inflated — it can only be wrong about who worked them.

24 per cent. That is the share of the yard’s real labour that is done by people who appear in no ledger, hold no line in the patrons book, and are owed nothing by it. The boards they feed are all funded. They are not.

the district ending it is arithmetic, not sentiment

Put them in the book. That is the whole operation. Enter the farmer class at the same rate as everybody else and the shortfall stops being invisible — and then it is a real decision.

the honest optionsa crew of 44 instead of 20, or 41,189 hours of commitments cut. Both are real answers. Pretending the hours were free is not one.
And this is the same operation the archetype board describes from the other end. b holds the knowledge and a holds the title, and every accusation in that record turns on b having no standing. A district is standing. It is what turns b into somebody with a line in the book, which is the only thing that was ever missing.
And it will cost. The yard is not richer for having counted. It is 41,189 hours poorer than it thought, and it was always that poor — the difference is that somebody was carrying it uncounted. Elevation is not a gift here. It is the recognition of a debt that was already being paid by the wrong people.
