Why it is a chain and not three switches

Every rule that lights a pair names the standing order first and the release second — .board:has(#o-4:checked):has(#r-c:checked) .g3. There is no rule anywhere that lights a pair on a release alone. That is the whole enforcement: not a check that runs and could be skipped, but a selector that has nothing to match when the authority above is absent. Withdraw the standing order and four pairs go dark in the same frame, and no code ran to make it happen.

And a pair is genuinely atomic here. There is no per-hull control to abuse, because there is no per-hull control at all — the two hulls in a lane share one checkbox and one counter increment of hulls 2. A guarantee you cannot express in the interface is the only kind that cannot be worked around.
