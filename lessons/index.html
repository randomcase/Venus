Four Lanterns

Lessons in game development, in the order the machine actually needs them: a surface, a clock, a swarm, and a line to somewhere else. Four lanterns carry them. The fifth light is whoever is reading.

For four corporals, Vietnam. Each lesson carries one of them. Their names are not in this file and were not invented — they are four blank fields in lanterns.js, and filling them in updates every page here.

Why fireflies and why they are in this forest

Nine thousand square miles of forest terraform this planet, and the fireflies are part of the forest — not decoration on it. They are the only part of it that keeps time. A firefly runs its own clock, and when it sees another flash it moves its own clock a little towards that one. Nobody is in charge, nobody is counting, and within a few hundred cycles a wood full of separate lights is beating together.

That is a game loop. It is also the cheapest coordination there is: one signal per flash, no memory of anybody else, and no leader to lose. Everything in these four lessons is that one idea taken further — drawing it, timing it, coupling it, and finally moving it off your machine onto one somebody else can reach.

What is actually being taught

Not a framework and not an engine. Four things that are true of every game anyone has ever written, in the order they bite:

1. A surface is not a document. You clear it and redraw it, every frame, forever. Nothing you drew last frame is still there and nothing remembers it for you.
2. Never trust the frame rate. Simulated time and wall time are different quantities, and a game that confuses them runs at a different speed on every machine it will ever be played on.
3. Update and draw are different jobs. If drawing changes state you have a bug that only appears when the window is out of focus, and you will not find it.
4. Whoever owns the state owns the game. A client that computes its own truth will disagree with every other client, politely, forever.

The fifth light

Four lanterns and then whoever is holding the file. That is not sentiment, it is how the coupling rule works: a firefly only ever moves the clock of something close enough to see it. Lesson four is the one where the light leaves your machine, which is why it is last and why it is the one written in Java.
