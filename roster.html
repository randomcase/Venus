What it cost

sick berth unattended

Engine room right. Ferris travels alone.

Two rated hands below, which is what the oiler needs to sail, and the only person aboard who could have watched a splinted leg for ninety-eight days is standing a watch on the plates instead.

He is checked when somebody remembers. On the eleventh day the leg is swollen and nobody aboard knows whether that is expected. It is a long way to the hospital hull and the relay does not carry a doctor.

engine room one hand short

Ferris has his attendant. The engine has Brannock alone.

One rated hand below where the bill says two. He can run the plant; he cannot run it and sleep. On a ninety-eight-day passage that is not a staffing gap, it is a man being asked to be awake for three months, and the failure will not announce itself as a manning decision when it comes.

Ferris arrives with the leg set clean. Brannock arrives grey.

engine unqualified · sick berth unattended

You split the difference and lost both.

Vane is below, so the sick berth is empty. Brannock is not, so the second engine billet is filled by somebody who has never stood one — on the fleet's oiler, the hull the whole train draws from.

Two stations compromised to protect neither. This is the arrangement that looks most balanced on the bill and is the worst of the four.

engine has no rated hand at all

Nobody rated is below.

Vane is in the sick berth and Brannock is somewhere else. The engine room of an oiler is being run by people who are willing, which is not the same as people who are able.

She may well arrive. That is what makes this the decision that gets repeated.

the one person who could choose was not used to choose

Vane is on deck or on the key.

The whole bill turned on where she went, and she went to a station four other people could have stood. The engine room and the sick berth are both being covered by whoever was left.

There is no reading of this roster where that is the best use of her. She was the only person on the list who could be the answer to either question.

The finding

The roster is short by one person and that person is Vane. Six hands and six billets balance on paper, and the balance is a lie: two of the six billets need a rating only she and Brannock hold, and one of them needs training only she has. There is no assignment of these six people that mans the engine room properly and puts a trained attendant in the sick berth. You were not choosing the right answer. You were choosing which failure to own.

Why the page let you commit anyway. Because that is what happens. The bill balances, the numbers look right, the sailing time is fixed, and the gap only becomes visible when somebody is needed in two places — by which point the decision was made weeks ago by a person counting heads. A watch bill that balances in numbers and not in people is the most ordinary way a ship gets into trouble, and it never looks like a mistake at the moment it is made.

What would actually fix it: a seventh hand, or Ferris not travelling. Both are decisions somebody above this desk has to make, and neither of them is on this form. That is also true of most rosters.
