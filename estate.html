The upkeep

Buying the castle is the cheap part. This works out the other part from the fabric rather than from a rule of thumb, and then checks the two against each other.

Read this first. Every rate below is an assumption and is printed where you can see it. They are order-of-magnitude figures from commonly quoted ranges — not appraisals, not quotations, and not advice. What the page is actually for is the shape of the answer: which costs dominate, how the ceilings on each revenue model compare to the rates, and at what size the arithmetic stops closing. Those conclusions survive quite large errors in the inputs. The dollar totals do not.

the methodFrom the fabric, not from the value

The usual figure is one to three per cent of value, annually. It is unfalsifiable, because it rests on a number nobody can establish — what is Malbork worth? There is no comparable and no buyer.

So this goes the other way. The plans already declare metres of wall, wall height, ward dimensions and keep height. Those give square metres of pointing, square metres of roof and cubic metres to heat, and those have trades and prices attached. Then, at the end, the fabric-derived total is divided by two per cent to see what value the rule of thumb would have implied — and where that implied value is absurd, one of the two methods is wrong.

the four modelsThe ceiling matters more than the rate

Each model is costed the same way: what one unit earns, and how many units a year the fabric and the location actually permit. A castle charging $10,000 a night that fills nine nights has a smaller business than one charging $900 that fills two hundred, and prospectuses quote the rate.

the finding

I expected the break to be at size and the arithmetic refuses it. Neuschwanstein at 0.5 hectares and Malbork at 12.5 — 23 times the area — both close, and they close on the same model. The one that fails on every model is Krak des Chevaliers at 4.2 ha, which is in the middle of the range.

Every plan that closes, closes on the gate. Not on hospitality, not on weddings, not on film. Those three have hard ceilings in units — there are only so many Saturdays, and a bigger hall does not create more of them — so their maximum take is fixed no matter what you charge. Ticketing is the only model whose ceiling scales with anything at all.

And what it scales with is not the building. It is the road. Krak des Chevaliers fails because 12,000 people a year can reach it. Malbork is 3.0 times larger, costs 2.5 times as much to keep, and closes comfortably because 550,000 people come. The constraint was never the rate and it was never the acreage.

Two corollaries, and they are the useful part if you are actually looking. Buy for the road before you buy for the building — the access figure is the only input in this whole model that sets a ceiling rather than a rate, and it is the one you cannot change afterwards. And buy the smallest thing that does the job, because every additional hectare adds outflow the day you sign and adds revenue only if it adds units, which it almost never does.

The reason the largest castles are publicly held turns out not to be about size either. It is that the ones worth keeping are frequently the ones nobody can conveniently reach, and a state can hold a building whose ceiling is zero. A private owner cannot, whatever the rate card says.

Where it comes in far under, the fabric method is probably over-charging somewhere. The two methods agreeing within a factor is the most either of them earns.

and the one that is not on EarthThe Deck Keep

Its outflow computes to $991k a year on the same rates, and every one of those rates is wrong for it. There is no lime mortar at fifty-five kilometres, no roofer, no insurer writing like-for-like on a pressure vessel above Venus, and no visitors at all — the only model with a ceiling that scales is the one model that is unavailable.

Which is the honest end of this document. Every private model here converts a building into a venue, and a venue needs people who can get there. The deck cannot be a venue, so it cannot be paid for this way, and that is not a gap in the analysis — it is the analysis. It has to be held the way the largest castles on Earth are held: by somebody who has decided the thing should exist and is not asking it to pay.
