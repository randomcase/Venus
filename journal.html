The rename

Write 300–400 words in ONE of these voices, at a specific hour on a specific day. No summary of the case, no verdict on it, no anachronism. The voice must know things the other three do not, and must not know things they do.

voice · voice-rename
Johnson v. M'Intosh, US Supreme Court, 1823. A title dispute over land in what is now southern Illinois. Thomas Johnson's heirs held land bought directly from Piankeshaw sellers in 1773 and 1775; William M'Intosh held a later federal patent on overlapping ground. Chief Justice Marshall, for a unanimous court, held that discovery had vested title in the discovering European nation, that the United States had inherited it, and that native nations retained only a 'right of occupancy' — which meant they could not sell to a private buyer, because they had never held the thing being sold. Nobody was dispossessed by the ruling on the day it was handed down. The land had been renamed, and the renaming was the whole of it.
