The Waterway

A healthy one, which is not a clean one. It has to get dirty on purpose, in a specific place, or it poisons itself with its own cleaning — and while I was working that out it moved the whole settlement five kilometres up.

What this page taught me while I built it teach as you learn

The forest was sited at 50 km, because that is where the pressure is one atmosphere and that is the number everybody quotes. Then I worked out the dissolved oxygen, and the site moved.

at 55 km · 27 °C7.9 mg/LHealthy. Anything can live in it.
at 50 km · 75 °C2.6 mg/LHypoxic. Below what most fish survive.
the cost0.53 barAmbient pressure given up. Engineerable.
the gain+5.3 mg/LNot engineerable without spending energy forever.

So the waterway picks the altitude. Not the pressure, not the buoyancy, not the view — the oxygen budget of the water. Warm water holds less gas, and 75 °C water holds almost none. That was not obvious to me when I sited it, the arithmetic overruled the plan, and the terraform page was written on the older assumption. Both are still up. This one is right.

The six stages build the loop and see what it does

What the water is doing counted from the panels, not declared

Oxygenatedneeds riffle

Gas is going in. Turbulence puts oxygen into water and nothing else does it for free — every alternative is a machine running on power you have to keep supplying.

Settledneeds pool

Solids are dropping out and the water is spending long enough in one place to be worked on. Residence time is the quiet variable: too fast and nothing is processed, too slow and it goes anoxic where you did not want it to.

Nitrifyingbed · riffle

Ammonia to nitrite to nitrate, by bacteria living in the bed. It needs oxygen, which is why it needs the riffle: the bed does the work and the riffle pays for it. Ammonia is the toxic one, so this stage is the difference between a waterway and a hazard.

Denitrifyingbed · anoxic pocket

Nitrate back to nitrogen gas, and out. This only happens where there is no oxygen, which is why the pocket has to exist and be left alone.

The two halves cannot share water. One needs oxygen and the other needs its absence, so a working system runs both in different places at the same time. The waterway cleans itself and dirties itself, deliberately, and neither zone is the healthy one.

Closedcondense · siphon

What comes out goes back. On a planet where hydrogen is the only scarce element, every litre lost is a litre that has to be wrung out of about 136,000 m³ of sulfuric acid cloud. The loop is not tidiness. It is the cheapest water there is by an enormous margin.

Healthyall six

Oxygenated, settled, nitrifying, denitrifying and closed. Note what healthy required: a stage whose whole job is to have no oxygen in it, and a stage whose whole job is to be turbulent and loud. Neither is the good one. The alternation is the good one.

And it is at 55 km, at 27 °C, holding 7.9 mg/L — because at the altitude everyone quotes it would be at 2.6 and none of the above would work regardless of how well it was plumbed.

Stagnantpool, no riffle

All settling and no aeration. The pool consumes the oxygen it arrived with, nothing replaces it, and the water goes anoxic where you needed it aerobic. This is what a pond becomes when nothing disturbs it, and it is the failure that looks most like success from a distance: still, clear, and dead.

Nitrate accumulatingbed and riffle, no anoxic pocket

Half a nitrogen cycle. Ammonia is being converted, which feels like the system working, and the nitrate has nowhere to go — it builds up indefinitely because the only thing that removes it needs an oxygen-free zone you did not build.

This is the instructive one. Every reading is better than it was: ammonia down, oxygen high, water clear. The failure is invisible in exactly the measurements you would take to check for it, and it is caused by removing the part of the design that looked unhealthy.

A dead spotanoxic pocket, no bed

An oxygen-free zone with no bacteria in it is not a denitrifying stage, it is just somewhere the water goes bad. The pocket only works as part of the bed — the biology is the mechanism and the absence of oxygen is merely the condition it needs.

Open loopcondensing, not returning

Water is being made and not given back. This works perfectly and it works until the reservoir is empty, at which point the cost per litre becomes the cost of sweeping cloud. An open loop is a closed loop with the failure deferred to whoever is on shift when it runs out.

Dry channelnothing built

A cut in the ground with no water in it. Everything above is a stage in a process and none of them is the process — which is worth seeing once, because the temptation with a list of six things is to believe the list is the system.

Milligrams per litre at saturation, fresh water. Above about 6 the demanding species are fine; below 5 most fish are in trouble; below 2 is hypoxic. Warm water cannot hold oxygen and no amount of plumbing changes that — it is a property of the solubility of a gas, so the only lever is temperature, and on Venus temperature is altitude.

Biosphere 2 the one that already tried it

3.14 acres, eight people, sealed in 1991 for two years. It is the only serious attempt at a closed ecology at any scale and it should be named on every page like this one rather than quietly not mentioned.

The oxygen fell from 20.9% to 14.2% in about sixteen months — the equivalent of moving everyone to four thousand metres — and it had to be injected from outside. The cause was not exotic and that is the point. Soil microbes respired faster than the plants photosynthesised, and the curing concrete absorbed the CO₂ so the carbon never came back round to be re-fixed. Two ordinary processes, neither hidden, both left out of the model.

The soil you install is metabolically active and it eats. This forest's entire floor — the beetles, the burial, the bed this waterway filters through — is that same process, deliberately installed and deliberately fed. It is the right design and it is also exactly the thing that took Biosphere 2's oxygen, and both of those are true at once.

No moon why the siphon is not a detail

Venus has no satellite. Not a small one — none. That removes a piece of infrastructure Earth has never had to think about, because it has always been there and nobody installed it.

Tides are a mixing engine running on nothing. Earth dissipates on the order of 3.7 terawatts in tidal friction, most of it lunar, and the visible half of that is an enormous volume of water moved twice a day, everywhere, forever, at no cost to anybody. Estuaries flush. Coastal water turns over. Oxygen goes down and nutrients come up because the Moon is there.

Venus gets none of it. The Sun raises a tide, but the planet turns once every 243 days, so nothing is stirred at any useful rate. Every litre that moves on Venus moves because something paid for it.

Which makes the siphon load-bearing rather than clever. It is the only free mover available: a height difference and 0.9 g, no pump, no power, self-priming once started. On Earth a designer can be sloppy about circulation because the Moon covers the difference. Here the circulation budget is exactly what you build and not one joule more, and a loop that quietly relies on something stirring it will find that nothing does.

The Moon also holds Earth’s tilt steady at 23.4 degrees, within about one, for billions of years; the modelling says that without it the axis wanders across tens of degrees. Venus is tipped 177 degrees and turning backwards. No seasons worth the name, no tides, and a day that depends on which altitude you are standing at. A place where nothing moves on its own is the honest one-line specification for this whole project.

Learn as you teach what this page does not know

Nobody has run a closed water loop at this scale. The ISS recovers most of its water and does it for six people with consumable filters resupplied from Earth. Biosphere 2 was three acres and lost its oxygen. This is twenty-three thousand square kilometres, and the honest position is that the stages above are correct in kind and completely unvalidated in size.

What is solid: warm water holds less oxygen, nitrification needs oxygen, denitrification needs its absence, siphons work at 0.9 g, and soil respires. Every one of those is school-level and none of them is in doubt. What is not solid is every number that would follow from putting them together at scale, and this page does not print those numbers rather than printing them softly.
