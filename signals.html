Signal Code

Four lamps — red, yellow, blue, green — and every one of the sixteen states they can hold, sorted by the thing a person reads first: how many are lit. no script
0000
no signal

Dark

Nothing lit. The dangerous state, because it is indistinguishable from a dead panel — a ship with nothing to say and a ship that has lost the ability to say it look identical from outside. Every other state on this page is safer than this one.

1000
aspect

Stop

Held. Not a speed — a different kind of thing entirely, which is why it is one lamp on its own and never a qualifier.

0100
aspect

Slow

Under way and short of cruise. Something is in the way; this is not the economical setting, it is the constrained one.

0010
aspect

Cruise

The setting a hull should sit on for a long lane. Slow, and slow on purpose — the only aspect that is meant to be held indefinitely.

0001
aspect

Full

Everything she has, for as long as it takes and no longer. A request, not a state — nothing crosses ten light years green.

1100
transition

Coming to a stop

Slowing under control, will be stopped shortly. The ordinary way a hull stops: red is already showing so nobody is surprised by it.

1010
transition

Holding on station

Stopped, but stopped somewhere on purpose — alongside a stage, taking on fuel or stores. Under way again without a new order.

1001
transition · emergency

Crash stop

Full and stop together, which is a contradiction and is meant to be: everything astern, now, damage accepted. The only state on this page that asks for a machinery casualty and gets one.

0110
transition

Rejoining cruise

Coming up off slow to the economical setting. The obstruction is cleared and she is settling back onto the lane.

0101
transition

Working up

Slow, going to full, skipping cruise. Boilers or bank coming on line — the interval where power is arriving but is not there yet.

0011
transition

Released

At cruise with standing authority to go full without asking. The only two-lamp state that is a permission rather than a movement.

1110
degraded · no green

No full power

She can stop, crawl and cruise; she cannot sprint. Least serious of the four, and the most common — a plant down a bank, not a casualty.

1101
degraded · no blue

No cruise

Stop, slow and full available; nothing in between that can be held. She can cross the lane but not economically, and she will arrive needing more fuel than she was budgeted.

1011
degraded · no yellow

No slow

No steerage speed below cruise. She cannot creep, so she cannot come alongside anything — a hull in this state can sail and can stop, and can do nothing careful.

0111
degraded · no red

No stop available

Three speeds and no brake. The most serious state in the code, and the reason the code names the missing lamp rather than the lit ones — a crew must hear this before they need it, not at the moment they reach for the stop and find it is not there.

1111
all lit

Test, or general alarm

Every lamp answering. Struck before departure to prove the panel, and otherwise meaning that everything is happening at once — which, at a signal panel, is the same message as nothing being legible.

The row of the triangle. 1 4 6 4 1 is the fourth row of Pascal's triangle, and it is not a coincidence that the code falls into it — the categories are the binomial coefficients because a signal is a subset of the lamps, and there are exactly C(4,k) subsets of size k. The design did not choose five categories; four lamps did.

Why sixteen and not four billion. Four lamps admit 4,294,967,296 possible functions from their states to a four-valued output — the arithmetic is in the other direction and it is enormous. This page uses sixteen, because the useful capacity of a signal panel is not how many states it can hold but how many a tired person can read correctly at three in the morning. Sixteen is already generous. The four single-lamp aspects carry almost all the traffic, and everything else on this page exists for the moments when the ordinary four are not enough.
