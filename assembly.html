The Assembly

Every tradition at one table, above a planet none of them could stand on. The first thing that fails at a table like this is not theology — it is the calendar. And the thing they agreed on anyway, without ever meeting, was a number none of them owned.

The problem there is no shared now

Six ways of counting a year, and no translation between them that does not privilege one. A council whose members cannot say when cannot schedule, cannot take a minute, cannot hold anybody to anything, and cannot even establish that two things happened at the same time.

This is the relay problem, exactly. Venus and Earth are between 38 and 261 million kilometres apart depending on where they are in their orbits, so a signal takes between about 2.1 and 14.5 minutes each way. There is no shared present tense to appeal to. A protocol that requires one is not slow. It is wrong.

The seats each keeps its own reckoning

Empty a chair and watch what stops being agreeable. Each agreement below is a conjunction: it names the traditions that share enough of a frame to settle that particular thing, and it disappears when either of them leaves. Except the last one.

What the table can agree on counted from the panels, not declared

What an hour isBabylonian · Greek

Sixty. The division of the hour into sixty minutes and the minute into sixty seconds is Babylonian sexagesimal arithmetic, carried into Greek astronomy and never dislodged since. You are still using it. It is the most successful piece of interoperability at this table and nobody at the table proposed it as a standard.

How long a year isEgyptian · Babylonian

Three hundred and sixty-five days, and a disagreement about the remainder. One of them ignores the quarter-day entirely and lets the calendar walk around the seasons; the other patches it by inserting a whole month when the drift becomes obvious. Both are coherent. Neither is wrong. They cannot both be used.

That the moon is a unitVedic · Babylonian

A month is a moon, and a year is an argument about how many moons make one. Both traditions solve it the same way — add a thirteenth month when the count falls behind — having arrived at the fix separately, because there are not many ways to reconcile 12 × 29.5 with 365.

That cycles nestMesoamerican · Egyptian

Both run a count that deliberately does not close, and both know exactly when it comes back. 260 against 365 realigns every 52 years; a 365-day civil year with no leap day walks the seasons and returns after about 1,460. Neither treats the drift as an error to be removed. It is a longer cycle, and it is tracked.

That a week is arbitraryNorse · Greek

One counts in weeks of nights and adds one when the year slips; the other does not use a week at all in the same sense and varies by city. Between them they demonstrate the useful thing: the week is the only unit at this table with no astronomical basis whatsoever. Nothing in the sky is seven days long.

Venusneeds no agreement at all

Five hundred and eighty-three point nine two days. The planet returns to the same position relative to the sun on that period, and it does so whether or not anybody at this table is speaking to anybody else.

The Babylonians wrote down its risings in the seventeenth century BC. The Maya built a table for it on a canonical 584 days and corrected it across centuries against the drift, using the resonance that makes it tractable: five Venus cycles is 2,920 days, and eight solar years is 2,920 days. Five to eight, almost exactly. None of these people were in contact with each other.

They arrived at the same number because the number was not theirs. That is the whole mechanism of this page and the answer to the relay. When you cannot share a clock, you do not negotiate one — you both watch something outside the room that neither of you owns, and you agree by arithmetic rather than by treaty. It is what the moths do with the moon. It is what anything on the far side of fourteen light-minutes has to do with a planet.

An empty tablenobody seated

Nothing is agreed, which is correct rather than sad: with nobody at the table there is nothing to agree between. Note that even the Venus row is dark. The period is still 583.92 days and the planet has not stopped — but a fact nobody is observing is not yet a shared reference, and the difference between those two things is the entire subject of the page.

The planet itself and it is stranger than the table

Synodic period583.92 d Back to the same place relative to the sun. The number every tradition here found separately.
Orbit224.7 d One year on Venus, measured against the stars.
Rotation243.0 d Longer than its year, and backwards. Venus turns the other way to almost everything else in the system.
Solar day116.75 d Sunrise to sunrise is shorter than the rotation, because turning backwards while going round adds up instead of cancelling.
Resonance5 : 8 Five Venus cycles, eight Earth years, 2,920 days each. The reason a 584-day table stays usable for centuries.
Signal each way2.1 – 14.5 min 38 to 261 million kilometres. No shared present tense, at any point, ever.

The day is longer than the year and the sun rises in the west. A planet where the clock runs slower than the calendar is a strange place to put a forest, and it is the place this whole yard has been terraforming. Worth keeping straight: the 243-day figure is the rotation against the stars, and the 117-day figure is sunrise to sunrise. Both are correct and they are not the same measurement, which is the third time on this page that two true descriptions of one object have been mistaken for a contradiction.

Hesperus is Phosphorus the oldest worked example

The morning star and the evening star are the same planet. Several traditions counted them as two for a long time, and reasonably: they appear at opposite ends of the day, months apart, and nothing about seeing them tells you they are one body.

The resolution is not that one of the names was wrong. Both were accurate descriptions of the same object seen from two sides of its own orbit — a thing that is and is not itself at the same time, which is the subject of theory.md and, it turns out, was overhead the whole time. The identity was an achievement, not a correction, and collapsing the two names into one lost nothing because the ambiguity was never load-bearing. That is the rare case. It is worth knowing what it looks like, so as not to expect it everywhere.

What is and is not on this page

These are traditions as calendars. The page names reckonings, cycles and observations that were actually recorded; it does not put words in anybody's mouth, retell anybody's scripture, or rank the six against each other. Six ways of counting a year and one planet they all counted, which is the only thing here anybody needs to have agreed on.
