Kill Switch

Red comes back — wired apart from the aspects, behind two actions. And the fourth lamp becomes the auxiliary channel: one lamp, four jobs, one at a time. no script
three aspects · one auxiliary

Aspect the lane setting

Three speeds, exactly as before. None of them is a stop and none of them ever was — that job does not live here any more.

Fourth lamp auxiliary channel

Tow — gold. She is on a line, or wants to be. The tender reads this and closes; everything else gives way.
Power — cyan. Plant fault or a bank down. She is under way but not at the aspect she is showing.
Medical — white. Someone aboard needs the hospital hull. Outranks cargo, outranks schedule, outranks the tariff.
Cut standing by — red. The switch is manned and someone has a hand near it. Not thrown; watched.

The cut two actions

Reversible. Arming says out loud that someone is about to do this, which is most of what it is for.
Not reachable until armed. Not reversible in any sense the lane cares about — everything stops where it is.
Safe. The cut is unarmed and the throw cannot be reached. Traffic runs on the aspect showing.
Armed. Nothing has happened yet and the lane has not changed. The difference between this state and the last one is entirely social: somebody has declared an intention.
Cut. Every hull stopped where it stood, the fourth lamp flashing red over whatever it was doing, and the aspect irrelevant — a speed is not an answer to a stop. Nothing on this page restarts the lane; that is deliberate and it is the whole point of a kill switch.

The failure it fixes. When red was one of four aspects it was overloaded — a signal, an order and a brake in the same control — so when it came off the panel all three went at once and nothing noticed. A stop that lives in the speed selector can be deselected by anyone choosing a speed. This one cannot be reached by choosing anything.

And why two actions rather than one. A single control that ends everything is a control somebody eventually leans on. Arming is reversible and announces itself; throwing is neither. Splitting them costs a second and buys the difference between an accident and a decision.

The omnitool, honestly what one lamp can carry

The fourth lamp does four jobs and shows exactly one at a time, in its own colour, and the state is legible at a glance from distance. That is the most an omnitool can honestly be. A lamp that meant four things simultaneously would mean nothing — you would have to ask, and a signal you have to ask about is not a signal. When the cut is thrown it takes the lamp over entirely, flashing, because at that moment nothing else the auxiliary channel had to say still matters.
