Sumer

The same twelve functions, itemised fifteen hundred years earlier by people who had never heard of Olympus and who wrote theirs on clay because they were running the actual thing. It matches almost everywhere. The page is about where it does not.

Why put them side by side the obvious objection

The Twelve argues that twelve sectors are what an economy cannot do without, and uses the Olympians because their domains are already attested. The fair objection is that I chose a list to fit an argument.

So: here is an independent list. Older, unconnected, and drawn up by the civilisation that was doing it first rather than describing it afterwards. Where two cultures who never met itemise the same functions, the list is doing less arguing than it looks. And where they disagree, one of them drew a distinction the other lost.

Nisaba the finding, and it is theirs rather than mine

The Twelve argues that writing is downstream of trade, on the evidence that cuneiform begins as barley tallies. That is an inference from the archaeology.

Sumer states it directly. Nisaba is the goddess of grain and the goddess of writing and the goddess of accounts, and she is one goddess. Not a grain deity who later acquired literacy: her domain is both from the earliest attestations, because to the people doing it, counting grain and writing were not two activities that needed relating. Scribes ended their tablets praising her, and they were praising the same power that made the barley.

Greece split her. Demeter got the grain and Apollo got the measure, and once they are two gods the connection between them is a thesis somebody has to argue rather than a fact of the pantheon. That is what a lost distinction looks like from the far side: not an error, just a question nobody has a reason to ask any more.

An and Enlil the other break, in the other direction

Anauthority in nameSky, highest rank, invoked in every list, and does almost nothing in any story.
Enlilauthority that actsWind and storm, the executive, the one whose word settles it and whose anger has consequences.
Zeusboth, mergedHighest rank AND the thunderbolt, in one figure, with no seam.

Sumer kept them apart and Greece welded them together, and the weld is where every constitutional argument since has happened. A crown that reigns and a cabinet that rules; a founding document and a court; a board and an executive. Every one of those is a society rediscovering that authority-in-name and authority-that-acts are different things, after inheriting a model in which they were the same god.

On the board, Zeus contributes nothing until Hera and Apollo are running — he needs a rule and a record. That is the An half showing through: rank with nothing to act on. The Enlil half is the part that does something, and it is downstream of everything.

The me a requirements list from four thousand years ago

Enki holds the me: the decrees, the itemised components of civilisation, which Inanna takes from him to Uruk. What is remarkable is not any single entry. It is that somebody thought civilisation was the kind of thing you could enumerate.

Three things about that list are worth taking. It separates authority, decision-making and judgment into three entries, which is the An/Enlil split again and finer than most modern org charts. It puts the crafts in individually rather than as "industry". And it includes fear and dismay — the costs are components, not side effects, which is more honest than any requirements document I have ever seen.

profiles.json is the same artefact: a list of what a thing needs, with statuses, because somebody decided the components could be written down and counted. Four thousand years apart and the format is recognisable.

Inanna is the planet and always was

Inanna is Venus. Not by later syncretism — in the earliest layers, the goddess and the morning and evening star are the same. The Venus Tablet of Ammisaduqa, which the assembly uses as the oldest observational record on the page, is a record of her risings and settings, kept because her appearances mattered and had to be predicted.

Which puts the whole of this yard in an odd position. The planet being terraformed is the one that pantheon put in the sky first; the 583.92-day period that every calendar-keeper found independently was found by watching her; and Hesperus and Phosphorus — the thing that is and is not itself — is her, seen from two sides of her own orbit, by people who had been counting her for a thousand years before anybody wrote the paradox down.
