Ophidia

Three serpents. The first explains who gets hurt, the second explains why cutting does not work, and the third explains why a body cannot check itself. The middle one is the best model of a capture-shape anybody has written down, and it is three thousand years old.

One · the cradle pair

the myth Hera sends two serpents into the nursery to kill the infant Herakles. He takes one in each hand and strangles them. His twin Iphicles screams and cannot. The story is told ever after as the first proof of what he was.
what it actually says Two children in the same cradle, the same threat, and one of them could answer it. That is not a story about strength — it is a story about who happened to have a defence and who did not, decided before either of them chose anything.

And the children who could not defend themselves were killed anyway, later, by him — when Hera sent the madness and there was nothing in the world arranged to stop the strongest man alive. Megara and their three sons had no interlock. Neither, at that moment, did he.

The line this yard keeps arriving at. A control that only works while the powerful party is sane is not a control. It is a hope with paperwork attached — and the cradle is where you can see that the difference between the protected and the unprotected was never about merit. 2B has two lamps and both of them mean yes.

Two · the Hydra

hands present
Three heads, none struck. Cutting is the obvious move and it is available to one person acting alone. That is exactly what makes it the wrong one.
Two grew back. The head is off and the stump opened two more — you are now worse off than before you struck, and you can do this all day. Cutting harder is the same move.
One pair of hands is not enough. Somebody is holding the torch and nobody is holding the blade, or the reverse. The stump is open and the moment passes — the searing has to happen while the cut is fresh, which is precisely why the myth needs two people and why one hero could not finish the second labour alone.
Seared. Nothing came back. The head did not stop it — denying the regrowth stopped it, and that took two people acting in the same instant. Herakles cut; Iolaus put the brand to the stump. It is the second labour, and Eurystheus refused to count it for exactly that reason: he had help.
the myth The Lernaean hydra grows two heads for every one struck off. Herakles cannot win by cutting. His nephew Iolaus follows him with a burning brand and sears each stump as it opens, and only then does the thing die. Eurystheus discounts the labour afterwards because Herakles did not do it alone.
what it actually says Three findings, and every one of them is in this yard already.

One — the visible part is not the mechanism. Heads are what you can see and reach, and removing them accelerates the problem. Prosecuting the courier does not touch the office that issued the folder.

Two — the win condition is denying regrowth, not landing hits. Cauterising is unglamorous, it produces no trophy, and it is the entire solution. Every remedy on the indicators page is a cautery: recompute so a figure cannot be taken on trust again, name the exception so the next waiver has a precedent, publish a claims window so silence stops being free.

Three — it takes two, and the tradition punished him for it. One person cannot cut and sear in the same instant. That is dual control described as a labour, and the oldest recorded objection to it is Eurystheus': that does not count, you had help. It counts. It is the only thing that ever worked.

Three · the ouroboros

the myth The serpent that takes its own tail in its mouth. Complete, closed, and sustaining itself out of itself — drawn on Tutankhamun's shrine, copied into Greek alchemy, and read ever since as eternity, renewal, and the unity of a thing with its own end.
what it actually says, here It is also the exact picture of a body that audits itself. Closed, and nothing outside it. Nobody is claiming the ouroboros is corrupt — that is the point. It is perfectly self-consistent, which is a different property from being right, and the difference is invisible from inside.

A ledger that recomputes itself agrees with itself every time. That is why the bridge recomputes from the inputs instead of believing the total, why the vault needs two keys rather than one careful holder, and why the powers block names who may audit as a required field. Not because anybody is assumed dishonest. Because a closed loop cannot tell.

One thing worth saying plainly. None of these is a story about a monster. The cradle pair were sent, the hydra was guarding a spring, and the ouroboros is not doing anything to anybody. What each one describes is a shape — an arrangement that produces an outcome regardless of anyone's intentions. That is why the answers are all structural and none of them is courage.
