The run two out, one back

Two to one, each cargo, in and out. Every cruiser under way carries two units out to land two and brings one back. The other one stays there — that is not loss and it is not waste, it is the point of the run. Land two is ten light years from anywhere and the cargo sits on it; the second unit is what is being left behind on purpose, and it holds there for as long as the lane is open.

Aspects, and why blue is not the slow one. Yellow means she is being held up by something ahead. Blue means she is on cruise: the economical setting, the one a hull sits on for a lane this long, and the one the board expects to see. Green is full power and it is a request, not a state — nothing on a ten-light-year run stays green. Red is not a speed at all.

The eighth hull. Station four's second lane is a repair tender, and nobody ordered her onto this run. She is here because a lane this long is where things break, and a tender that keeps station with cruisers is escorting them whether or not anyone wrote it down. She is an auxiliary doing escort work by accident, which is most of what auxiliaries end up doing.

The lane is a CSS animation and the aspect is its duration. Every stop is animation-play-state: paused applied from a :has() further up the tree, and the four figures above are CSS counters. Nothing here is scripted, nothing is transmitted, and the ten light years are a label on a div.
