What is editable, and what only looks it. The three bands under each stage are real: pick one and every figure in the ledger below moves, because the rule that charges a hull names both her aspect and that stage's band. The stage names are typed directly into the page and are yours to change — they are labels, and changing one moves nothing. A free-typed tariff would need JavaScript to reach the arithmetic, and there is none in this file; bands are how you get a customisable price with a total that stays honest.

Total take, this state of the board

An auxiliary at every stage, and every one of them on a tariff. A cruiser under way pays 730 across the four stages — fuel at two light years, water at four, repair at seven, stores at nine. None of the four is doing the fleet a favour: they are stationed there because the traffic passes them, and traffic that passes you is revenue. That is why the stages exist at all, and it is the only reason a hull can fuel ten light years from anywhere.

Held hulls still pay. A cruiser at red pays 240 in demurrage to whichever stage she is sitting at. She is occupying a berth she is not moving through, and the stage is entitled to charge for it. So the board earns whether the lane runs or not — 730 a hull moving, 240 a hull stopped.

Which is the problem, and it is deliberate. If a stage earns from a hull it is holding, a stage has a reason to hold hulls. That is why no stage on this lane can set an aspect: the four aspects belong to the switchboard, the station stops belong to the four operators, and the master stop belongs to the board. An auxiliary can charge you, refuel you, tow you and invoice you — it cannot stop you. The syndicate takes its cut at every stage and has no hand on any switch, and that separation is the only thing standing between a profit motive and a lane full of held ships.

The run two out, one back

Two to one, each cargo, in and out. Every cruiser under way carries two units out to land two and brings one back. The other one stays there — that is not loss and it is not waste, it is the point of the run. Land two is ten light years from anywhere and the cargo sits on it; the second unit is what is being left behind on purpose, and it holds there for as long as the lane is open.

Aspects, and why blue is not the slow one. Yellow means she is being held up by something ahead. Blue means she is on cruise: the economical setting, the one a hull sits on for a lane this long, and the one the board expects to see. Green is full power and it is a request, not a state — nothing on a ten-light-year run stays green. Red is not a speed at all.

The eighth hull. Station four's second lane is a repair tender, and nobody ordered her onto this run. She is here because a lane this long is where things break, and a tender that keeps station with cruisers is escorting them whether or not anyone wrote it down. She is an auxiliary doing escort work by accident, which is most of what auxiliaries end up doing.

The lane is a CSS animation and the aspect is its duration. Every stop is animation-play-state: paused applied from a :has() further up the tree, and the four figures above are CSS counters. Nothing here is scripted, nothing is transmitted, and the ten light years are a label on a div.
