What it costs to be last read this one twice

The end that can see the problem is the end with no word for it. The outpost is the only party that knows whether it can take what is coming — whether the berth is clear, whether the lift margin is there, whether the last consignment already filled the hold. It has two lamps and both of them mean yes.

And the word would arrive too late anyway. One-way light time between the planets runs from 2.1 minutes at closest approach to 14.3 at furthest. Even if 2B had a red lamp, a refusal sent the moment a ship appeared on the scope would reach Earth long after the ship reached the outpost. The vocabulary is not the binding constraint. The vocabulary is an honest description of a constraint that already existed.

Which is why the whole burden sits at B2. Everything downstream is a lane with no brake in it — exactly what the cruiser board became the day red came off, and the reason the cut had to be wired back in as its own control rather than as a fourth aspect. A system with one red light is not underspecified. It is telling you, precisely, where the decision has to be made: before departure, by the only party that can still change its mind.

Set B2 to stop and watch the other two legs go dark. They do not disagree, they do not queue, they do not hold an opinion — there is simply nothing for them to pace. That is not deference. It is the arithmetic of being downstream.

Where the codes are written out

B2's sixteen states, each with a job, are on Signal Code — including the four degraded states named by the lamp that is missing. P2P's eight are on Three Lamps, which also shows what is lost when a lamp comes off: half the code, and the entire ability to report a fault. 2B's four are the trivial remainder — dark, cruise, full, both — and only two of them are orders.
