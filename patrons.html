The patrons

who pays, and what for

Every board in this yard costs somebody hours. This says which somebody, how many, and — because it is the only question that matters and the easiest one to skip — what the civilization gets in return. A sponsorship that cannot answer that last one is refused at build time and never reaches the page.

the book priced in deck hours, because hours are the one unit nobody can print

58,140 deck hours funded against 58,440 that exist — 99% of the crew’s year. A crew of 20 on an 8-hour day is 58,440 hours and not one more, and patrons.mjs refuses the whole book if the funded total goes over.
It did, on the first run. The patrons between them asked for 76,700 hours — 131% of a crew that does not exist. Trimming the asks until they fitted would have made this page tidy and destroyed the only figure on it worth having, so both are kept: 18,560 hours of work were asked for and are not being done. That is what a shortfall looks like when somebody writes it down instead of rounding it off.
The allocation is a decision and reads as one. Water and the wardrobe are funded whole because people die without them. The base and the instruments are funded whole because they are cheap and everything else leans on them. The entire cut falls on four discretionary lines, and the tables below say by how much for each.

Caio Veyron

King of the Guildsking×3.40 on book

the long hall, Habitation One

A king of the guilds and not of Venus, which is a distinction this yard settled before he existed. Venus has a president and more than a few judges; what it has no mechanism for is a crown over the whole of it. What it does have is guilds, and a guild answers to somebody. In this yard’s vocabulary the decentralized are the kings, so a king is exactly a figure with no power to compel and a great deal of power to convene — which is the only kind of king a place like this can run, and the only kind worth writing down.

the charm, as a number

×3.40

Charm is the multiple. He raises 3.4 times what his portfolio is worth on paper, on a signature and a room. That figure is the whole of the claim and it is the only part of it anybody should accept, because it can be checked and it can fall.

the face, as a function

on the mark

Handsomeness is recorded here because it is operational and not because it is flattering. A face gets onto the coinage or it does not, and his is: the mark carries his likeness. That is the oldest use a sovereign’s face has ever had and it is not decoration — a head on a coin is a maker’s mark, and it works by being recognised at a glance by people who cannot read. His does. Struck coin moves faster where the face is known, and the yard has the arithmetic for what that is worth and what it costs.

and what it costs

A likeness is memory. A coin that carries a face remembers who issued it, and memory is the one thing that ends fungibility — which is the treasury’s whole argument, arriving by way of a compliment. Putting a king on the mark makes the mark less like money and more like a signed object. That is a real price, and it is paid by everyone who holds one.

How to falsify it. Send the same proposal out under a clerk’s name, with the same numbers and no meeting. If it raises the same, the multiple was never his and the portfolio was simply good. If it raises the book value and no more, the multiple is real and it is a person rather than a document — which also means it is mortal, unhedged, and the single largest uninsured position in this layer.

Ilse Marnier

Mistress of the Fibre Guildprincess×1.10 on book

the spinning floor

Fungibility is the princesses’ department, and fibre is where the yard’s fungibility argument actually bites: a bale of one grade is interchangeable with any other bale of that grade, which is precisely what makes it tradeable and precisely what a house destroys when it stamps a serial on the finished object. She sits on the side of the trade that wants the bale to stay anonymous.

How to falsify it. Compare the two multiples in this layer. If a portfolio of similar quality raises 1.1 without a person and 3.4 with one, the difference is the person. If every portfolio raises 3.4, the market is loose and nobody is charming.

No patron

the unsponsored

The honest row. Most boards in this yard are paid for by nobody, and a patronage layer that quietly omitted them would be a brochure. What is unsponsored is not what is worthless — it is what nobody has yet had to justify. The board lists them by name.

Take anything on the unsponsored list and ask what breaks if it stops. If the answer is nothing, it should stop. If the answer is something, it has been running on goodwill, and goodwill is not a budget line.
