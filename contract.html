Form Completion Contract

A form is not complete when its boxes are full. It is complete when every check it carries has actually been performed — and checking costs something. This gives that cost a name, gives each desk a budget, and states what happens when they do not meet. Too heavy is a disposition, not a complaint.

Disposition

No execution without a disposition. The contract does not tell you the answer until you have committed to one. Executed. Entered against the form.

Complete.

The form is accepted and the record now says its checks were performed. That claim is only worth anything if the gas was actually there to perform them — which is the entire reason this contract bothers to count.

Returned to a desk that can afford it.

Not a rejection. The form is fine; this desk is not the one that can verify it, and saying so costs a day and a signature rather than a reputation.

Escalation is the answer the shape least wants, because a heavier desk is one it has not already learned to pass through.

Burned.

Refused, and the gas spent getting this far is forfeit. The form does not come back corrected because it cannot be — its checks depend on inputs that do not exist, and no budget buys a recomputation of figures whose sources are missing.

Burning is the disposition, not the temper. The alternative is a stamp that says checked on a document nobody could check, and that stamp is worth less than nothing: it converts an open question into a closed lie, and the next reader has no way to tell.

Read the syndicate row. It is heavy on purpose and that is not an accident of design — the shape's whole method is to make checking cost more than waving it through. A routine desk cannot afford to look at it, which is precisely why it keeps arriving at routine desks. The remedy is not a braver clerk. It is a budget the clerk is allowed to spend by escalating.

And read the last row twice. Nothing in the top four rows ever burns. Burning is reserved for a document that cannot be verified at any price — not one that is expensive, not one that is suspicious, and not one you dislike. Widening that definition is how a burn stops being a control and starts being a weapon.
