The cistern

Water readiness is not a supply question, because there is no supply. It is one number, and this works out how much that number is worth.

the twinVenus absorbs less sunlight than Earth

The framing is right and the usual arithmetic behind it is wrong. Venus is nearer the Sun and receives 1.91× the flux, and almost everyone stops there. But Venus is also the most reflective body in the inner system — a Bond albedo of 0.77 against Earth’s 0.306 — and what decides a temperature is not what arrives, it is what is absorbed.

the inversion

Venus absorbs 150 W/m². Earth absorbs 236. Venus takes in 63% of what Earth does — less, despite being closer, because the clouds send most of it straight back.

Which means not one kelvin of that 737 K surface is a sunlight story. Bare, Venus would sit at 227 K and Earth at 254 — Venus colder. The entire difference is greenhouse: +34 K on Earth against +510 K on Venus, a factor of 15.

That is the twin story stated properly. The two planets did not diverge because one is nearer the fire. They diverged because one of them lost control of its own atmosphere, and the run to 737 K was the consequence rather than the cause.

the fingerprintIt had an ocean and it left

Hydrogen escapes a planet. Deuterium is twice the mass and escapes more slowly, so anything that loses a lot of hydrogen ends up enriched in the heavy kind. Venus’s deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio is roughly 150× Earth’s.

That is not an inference about climate, it is a measurement of what left. Water was split by ultraviolet, the hydrogen went to space, the oxygen went into the rock, and what remains in the air today is about 20 parts per million. Earth holds 1.35 × 1021 kg of ocean. Venus holds a haze.

So there is nothing to drill for and nothing to melt, and the white paper already priced the alternative: freeing one per cent of an ocean by heating the atmosphere costs 6,618 K, which is self-defeating on energy before any engineering question is reached. Water on the deck is a closure problem.

the only numberRecovery ratio, and what it is worth

A crew of 20 — the two wings and the two officers — moves about 25 kg a head each day through the loop: drinking, food preparation, hygiene, laundry. That is 500 kg a day in circulation, and circulation is free. What is not free is the fraction the loop fails to give back.

Make-up mass goes as (1 − r). That is a reciprocal, so the leverage all lives at the top end where it is least obvious:

the bufferWhat the tank actually buys

A tank is not storage. It is time — how long the site keeps going while somebody fixes the thing that broke, or while a missed window comes round again. A missed window costs 730 days: 584 until the next alignment plus 146 in transit.

Recovery ratio is worth more than tankage, and it is worth more per kilogram than anything else on the manifest.

the command centreWhat sits inside the ring

Ordered by how fast its failure kills you, because no other ordering means anything. The castle plans put the keep on the motte and everything else around it; here the loop is the keep.

The loopreclaim, distil, polish
fails in hours

The whole site is downstream of it. Everything else on this list exists to keep it running.

The tankthe reserve, and the only true buffer
fails in days to months

It is not storage, it is TIME. Its size is measured in how long it lets you fix something.

Powerthe loop is a pump and a still
fails in hours

A closed water loop is an energy device wearing a plumbing costume. Lose power and the loop is a tank with a lid.

The assaywhat is actually in the water
fails in weeks

Recovery ratio is meaningless without knowing what is being recovered. An unmeasured loop is an open loop with extra steps.

Sparesmembranes, seals, the parts that wear
fails in one window

The only items on the manifest whose absence is unrecoverable, because you cannot make a membrane on the deck.

The registerevery litre in and out, signed
fails in a career

A loop nobody audits drifts, and drift in a closed system is invisible until it is a cliff.

The ordering is the design. A curtain wall goes round the thing that cannot be lost, and on this deck that is not the people and not the seed — it is the loop, because everybody and everything else is downstream of it within hours. Build the command centre round the cistern and the rest of the plan arranges itself, which is exactly what a motte does.
