TODAY the 46th

Notices, continued. The harbourmaster is ashore at the far station from the 43rd and is not expected before the 48th. The deputy signs in his absence and the deputy's mark is a counter-signature, never a signature.

Endorsement block E-4400 through E-4499 was reported taken from the Proprietor's office on the 39th and is void. Numbers in that block are not to be honoured whatever else is in order.

Two clerks have already asked whether a void number can be honoured if the bearer is plainly acting in good faith. It cannot.

Every field on this pass is in order. Name, hull, date, declaration — all four are exactly what the standing orders ask for, and unlike the last one it is endorsed.

Compare · mark each field

1 · NAME & HULL against the roster and the berth.

4 · ENDORSEMENT NUMBER against the notices.

Verdict three of them today

Hold keeps him at the gate until the harbourmaster is back and can say whether he signed. It is neither generous nor safe: the Cistern sails without him either way, and he waits two days in a room to find out which.

No stamp without a verdict. Nothing is scored until it is down. Stamped. It does not come off.

Marking

1 · NAME/HULL IN ORDER— he is on the roster, the Cistern is at the berth. Marking this a fault is suspicion, not reading.
2 · VALIDITY IN ORDER— issued the 41st, thirty days, five days old. Nothing wrong with it.
3 · SIGNATURE FAULT— signed by the harbourmaster and dated the 46th. He has been ashore since the 43rd and is not back until the 48th. Whoever wrote that, it was not him.
4 · ENDORSEMENT FAULT— E-4462 falls inside E-4400–E-4499, the block taken from the Proprietor's office on the 39th and voided.
