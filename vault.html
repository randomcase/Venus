The window

Sealed. Fewer than two keys are turned. One key is not a lesser amount of authority — it is none. The bolts have not moved and no amount of correctness on the part of a single holder will move them.
Quorum met · time lock holding. Two proper keys, turned by two proper officers, and the door does not move. This is the case the time lock exists for: everything is right except the hour, and the mechanism has no way to be persuaded that tonight is an exception.
Open. Two keys inside the window. The bolts are back, the dial has run, and a line is being written in the register below — which is the part that actually protects the reserve.

The reserve is the spare from the funded tier — 90,000 capacity against a 48,600 yard, which leaves 41,400 that is not doing anything and is therefore the only money that can absorb a bad year. A reserve being idle is not a reserve failing to work. That is the work.

Why it is built this way

Dual control is not distrust. A single keyholder can be threatened, blackmailed, or simply worn down, and every one of those attacks requires that the person be able to comply. Two keys removes the ability, which removes the reason to lean on anybody. The officers are safer for it — that is the point people usually miss about the control, and it is the more important half.

The time lock protects against the same night. Two keys can be gathered by patience or by force in a few hours. A lock that will not open before four in the morning cannot be reasoned with, cannot be shown a good cause, and does not know whose life is being threatened. Its uselessness in an emergency is precisely its value — a mechanism that could be overridden for a good enough reason is a mechanism that will be overridden for a reason that sounded good enough.

And the register is the real security. The bolts stop a wrong opening; the register makes a right one accountable. Note what the third and fourth entries above say — that the register keeper was absent, or that all three came at once. Neither is a violation. Both are the kind of fact you want written down at the time and read by somebody else later, because the openings that turn out to matter almost never look wrong on the day.
