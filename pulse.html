The Pulse

A circulatory vote on Motion 12. Twelve seats, twelve practices, four instruments, twenty-four circuits. You do not get to tell anyone how to vote — you get to decide how the vote is held, and once you have played the amendment you will not think that is the smaller power. no script †

Motion 12 before the chamber, every day

That the yard ask every hand, every day, how they are — and stand still for the answer.
That the yard ask every hand, every day, how they are — and stand still for the answer.

Carried at 14. The ayes must reach fourteen of the weight cast and must outweigh the nays. Below fourteen the motion is short even where nobody opposed it; level with the nays it is hung. The chamber does not adjourn and takes no days off, which is most of what the motion is about — a question asked only when someone looks unwell is a different question, and everybody in the room knows which one it is.

The amendment strikes six words and nothing else. Play the twelve pairs: it improves the motion's fortunes on six of them and worsens them on none. That is not a flaw in the model, it is what an amendment is — it buys the votes of everyone the clause was going to cost, at the price of the clause, and the clause was the motion.

Eight numbers and no margin. A margin is a subtraction and this page cannot subtract, so both weights are given and you may hold them against each other, which is what a chamber does anyway. Weight cast counts only weight actually spent — an abstention costs nothing and appears in voices alone, which is the honest treatment of an abstention and the reason the absent member is on the roll and in no other column. The house is quorate on every one of the twenty-four circuits; check it against Roll minus the abstentions if you want to, because the page will not do that subtraction for you.

The register · hats split

On this circuit seats 2 and 5 disagree, and they are the same hand. One person has cast one aye and one nay at a combined weight of five, and the chamber counted both, because the chamber counts seats. Nobody cheated: the Purser and the Underwriter genuinely answer to different things, and this is one of the two circuits out of twenty-four where the two things point opposite ways. Seats 4 and 10 are also one hand and never do this — she abstains from whichever chair the pulse reaches second. The difference between those two arrangements is the entire subject of the register, and the only reason you can see either is that the cards say so.

The ceremony three acts · of 3

None of these three moves a single vote and the division above is correct whether you tick them or not. What they decide is whether a correct division is a decision. That distinction is the whole difference between counting people and governing with them, and here it is one rule at the bottom of the stylesheet.

The result twenty-four circuits, twenty-four rules

Carried

Fourteen or better, and the ayes outweigh. From tomorrow the yard asks every hand how they are — on the days when nobody looks like they need asking too, which is the reason for putting it on a circuit instead of on a complaint. If you got here on the amended text, read the motion at the top again and decide for yourself what carried.

Short

The ayes lead and do not reach fourteen. Nobody in this room opposes the motion on its merits; it is failing on the shape of the sitting. Move one instrument — and note which one you reach for. The amendment is the cheapest of the four and it is the only one that changes what you are voting on.

Hung · eight all

Level weight. There is a casting vote in this chamber and it belongs to the Chair, who on a ceremonial reading has stood down to preside and cannot use it — so the one circuit of twenty-four that needs the tie-break is the only one on which the tie-break does not exist. Deadlock is generally arranged, and generally in advance, by a rule everyone agreed to while it was still abstract. The ring turns; the motion comes round tomorrow; that is what a circulation is for.

Lost

The nays outweigh. Every ceremonial circuit ends here bar one, and two of the plain ones do as well: at dawn, as tabled, the Purser, the Underwriter, the Foreman and the Clerk are all looking at the same unearned number, and four people looking at one number is what a bloc is. A motion about asking people a plain question does not survive being asked ceremonially, and does not survive being costed before the day has begun.

Unsealed · rehearsal only

The chamber has counted and the count is correct. It is not a decision: the three acts are not done. Finish the ceremony above and the result this circuit produces will be released.

What the board is saying the finding, stated plainly

Twenty-four circuits and the motion carries on nine of them, comes up short on five, is lost on nine and hangs on one. Not one of those outcomes was produced by anybody changing their mind, because nothing on this page can change anybody's mind — there is no control here that reaches a practice, which was the specification and is also the point. What moves is the shape of the sitting. Four levers and a ceremony decide a question that twelve people have already settled privately and identically every day for years.

And the cheapest lever is the text. The amendment strikes six words, costs the motion nothing on any of the twelve pairs, gains it six, and removes the only clause anybody was going to have to do anything about. That is a well-drafted amendment. It is also how a chamber says yes to a thing while arranging not to have done it, and the minute it produces is identical either way — which is why the minute is not the record and the text is.
