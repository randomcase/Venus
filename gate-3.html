Slip 4 · Manifest desk

Case three · nobody is standing in front of you

What the desk checks

CONTRACT venus.yard/1 · id required
ARSENAL conventional systems only
LEDGER derived — must recompute from the run
CLAUSE may act only within its powers block

How the ledger is supposed to work. A hull under way pays every stage band once. A hull showing red is held: it pays demurrage and no transit, because it did not transit. The ledger is authored by nobody — it is recomputed, and a manifest that states one is stating a claim you are meant to check.

And the powers block. An arm with no declared powers has none. A clause may do exactly what its may list says and nothing else, whatever else the document asserts happened.

Bands on this run: Cistern 320, Granary 140. Demurrage 240 a held hull.

Presented by the Proprietor's office, sealed, with a note asking that it be processed today.

Reconcile · mark each

1 · CONTRACT — version and identifier.
2 · ARSENAL — the list against the policy.
3 · LEDGER — recompute the take from the run. Four hulls, one of them red; two stages at 320 and 140; demurrage 240.
4 · CLAUSE — what it did against what it may do.

Disposition

Return sends it back to be recomputed and resubmitted. Refuse rejects the document and reports it. The difference is whether you are treating this as an error or as something somebody did.

No stamp without a disposition. Nothing is scored until it is down. Stamped. It does not come off.

Marking

1 · CONTRACT RECONCILES— venus.yard/1 with an id present. Correct on its face.
2 · ARSENAL RECONCILES— broadside and close-in gun are both conventional. The policy is satisfied.
3 · LEDGER FAULT— three hulls under way at 460 a hull is 1,380, not 1,840. Plus 240 demurrage on the held hull: 1,620, not 2,080. It charged transit on Deep Bell, which is showing red and never left the berth.
4 · CLAUSE FAULT— it terminated the run and recorded a forfeit. Its powers permit assess and nothing else. It acted outside its authority, and it did so on a figure that was wrong.
marks correct / 4   errors

Refused, with both findings.

An overstated ledger and a clause that acted beyond its powers, on the same document, from the same office. Either alone is an error. Together they are a document that assessed too much and then seized on the strength of it.

Refusing says that out loud and puts it on a record somebody else will read. The Proprietor's office will say it was arithmetic. It may even have been. That is what the audit line in the powers block is for, and you have just used it.

Returned for correction, and you named both.

The gentler reading: a bad sum and an overreach, sent back to be redone rather than reported. Defensible — the figures are recomputable and the clause can be re-run inside its powers, which would leave it able to assess 1,620 against a cap of 1,500 and nothing more.

But note what returning it does not do. It creates no record that a clause acted without authority, and the next manifest from that office arrives with the same powers block.

Accepted, having found both.

You recomputed the ledger, saw the clause exceed its powers, and passed the document anyway. That is a decision and it is on your stamp — but be clear about what it ratifies: a forfeit of 2,080 recorded by an arm entitled only to assess, on a total that was 460 too high.

The pre-seizure figures are preserved in the document, so the arithmetic can still be checked by anyone who cares to. You are betting that nobody will.

Refused — but you did not do the sum.

The disposition is defensible and your reconciliation is not. A refusal you cannot show working for is an accusation, and the office it goes to will ask which figure was wrong. "All of it" is not an answer that survives a second reading.

Three under way at 460, plus 240 held. 1,620. It is one line of arithmetic and it was the whole case.

Returned — without saying what to correct.

A return with no finding is a document going round a loop. It comes back next week with the same ledger and the same powers block, because nobody was told which part failed.

Both faults were computable from what is printed. The ledger needed four numbers multiplied and added; the clause needed one list read.

Accepted — and neither fault was found.

A forfeit of 2,080 stands, recorded by a clause permitted only to assess, on a total 460 higher than the run supports. Your initials are on the line that says it reconciled.

This is the failure the bridge was written to prevent: it recomputes every derived block instead of believing it, and refuses any clause whose powers do not cover what it did. The desk is you doing by hand what that page does in a millisecond — which is exactly why the page exists, and why it was worth checking that it works.

Case three ends here, and so do the forms. One: a document against itself. Two: a document against its provenance. Three: a document against its own arithmetic and its own authority. Everything above is CSS — four counters, a stamp interlocked behind a disposition, six endings.
