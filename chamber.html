The Writing Chamber

An exchange of ideas, run as an exchange. The bench decides the case; the chamber issues it. Nine minds, three houses, and a listed instrument that is a sentence. no script

The issue one sentence, listed

That paying in was not voting, that dining was not ratifying, and that fifteen years of silence was fifteen years of a question nobody put.

The chamber does not care whether that is true. It prices it. Three levers set the price and none of them is the sentence: who signed it, how many sticks are bound to it, and which animal is loud in the room today. Play all twelve circuits and check — there is no circuit on this board where the quality of the idea appears in the arithmetic, because there is no line in the stylesheet where it could. That absence is the argument. It was not difficult to arrange.

The monkeys ape in: they buy the noise, they buy it late, and they are the only reason anything here has a price. The chickens do not buy, and are right about it later, at a moment when being right is worth nothing to anyone including them. This market needs exactly one of each and has never run without both.

One stick snaps in the hand of a child. Nine bound will not snap under a boot. It is the oldest political image there is, it has been carried by republics and by the worst governments of the last century alike, and neither of them was wrong about the mechanics.

The float is printed and not used. This exchange settles on heads. The stake column is there so you can see what settling on stake would mean: the fund is three hands of the nine and holds eight of the sixteen — half the float in three chairs — and every real exchange that has ever existed settles the other way. Settlement twelve circuits, twelve rules

Minted

Signed, and nine sticks bound to it. Six heads to three and the issue trades. Notice what did that: a name on the front and a bundle behind it. The sentence has not been read by anyone in this room since the levers were set, and it did not need to be.

Shorted

Per curiam. Nobody's name on it, so nobody's reputation in it, so no float — and it does not matter how many sticks you bind to an anonymous thing, five heads still take the other side. An unsigned good idea and an unsigned bad one price identically here, which ought to be the most alarming line on the board and is generally the least remarked.

Unbound · four all

One loose stick. Four heads each way, eight trading, and the ninth wallet is not one of them — she is not bound in, so she is not asked, so she does not trade, and the chamber cannot settle. All four of the circuits that end this way end this way for that reason. The signature does not save it and the animals do not save it. Bind the nine and it settles on the first attempt, which tells you exactly what was being priced.

What the board is saying since you asked for an opinion

An exchange of ideas is a lovely phrase and this is what one actually looks like when you build the mechanism honestly: three levers, none of them the idea. A syndicate does not vote — it matriculates funds upward and promotes from within, and being cyclical it reproduces, in bodies not identical but identitarianly aligned, mimicking the structure closely enough to be run the same way by the same hands. Less amoeba than germ. Cohesive as one organism, factional in its parts, and the factions are hats rather than people. There is no love in it, no sex, no survival. It is a cycle that learned to make another of itself.

The bundle is the whole tell. Nine sticks bound is a syndicate and it mints; one stick is a person and it snaps; and the ninth wallet only ever gets asked in the configuration where she is already bound in. That is not a market failure, it is the product. The single most useful thing on this page is the rule at the bottom of the stylesheet that stops a loose stick from minting, and the second most useful is that you can move it up three lines and watch the whole board keep counting, perfectly consistently, while quietly lying.

Every mind here is an archetype. Real people were proposed for these chairs and are not in them — the claim being made is that the chairs work the same whoever sits in them, and a named portrait is the one thing that would undermine it.

Where to start

The board opens on the Chief · one loose stick · monkeys, which is a signed idea that will not settle. Bind the nine and it mints on the first attempt without a single mind having changed position. Then set it per curiam and watch nine bound sticks fail to save an unsigned sentence. Three levers, twelve circuits, and the idea never appears in the arithmetic once.
