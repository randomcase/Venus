Decentralization conceptsettled

No participant reads any other. Removing one changes nothing for the rest.

Not "spread out", not "many copies", not "no company owns it". Those are consequences people notice and then mistake for the thing. The property is about READS: a system is decentralised exactly to the extent that no component's behaviour is a function of another component's state.

The automat is the cleanest instance in this yard because it was built to have nothing else in it. Each tribe keeps its own beat, off its own prime, and advances on beats that divide its own period. No tribe queries another. There is no coordinator, no scheduler with a policy, no leader election, no consensus round — the pattern you see on the board is not produced by anything, it is what falls out of several bands not talking.

Which is why removing one is a non-event. Delete a tribe and the remaining ones do not slow down, re-balance, or notice. That is not resilience bolted on afterwards; there is no failure mode to be resilient to, because there was never a dependency to break.

the test

Delete one component. If any other component's behaviour changes — timing, output, ordering, anything — it was not decentralised, and the coupling you just found is the real architecture.

Fungibility conceptsettled

No unit is worth more than another, and there is no way to write the function that would prefer one.

The test is constructive and it is the whole of the idea: try to write the preference. If you can write a function that ranks two units, they are not fungible, and the ranking is where the rent will be collected. If you cannot write it — not "choose not to", cannot — they are.

This is why the automat has no scoring function. Not as restraint, as a structural fact: there is no goal state to compare an arrangement against, so there is nothing for a ranking to be a ranking of. Every arrangement is reachable from every other and none is worth more, which means there is no arrangement anybody can be induced to reach and none anybody can be charged to leave.

The correction that produced this entry is worth keeping. Early in this repository's life the advice was to add a loss condition and a scarcity to make a game feel like a game. That advice was wrong in a specific way: fungibility exists to remove exactly those two things, and putting them back is not balancing a system, it is un-building it.

the test

Write the preference function over two units. If it compiles, they are not fungible. If it does not, ask what the syndicate would have to add before it could — that addition is the attack.

Not the most important thing. The smallest closed loop in which every part is fed by another part.

The instinct is to start with the root — the biggest, the most depended-upon, the one everything else needs. This yard tested that instinct and it fails, visibly, on a board you can open. Turn on Zeus alone in the twelve-sector economy: nought working, one broken. Add Hera: still nought, still broken. A root is not a start, because a root is defined by what depends on IT and a start is defined by what feeds IT. Those are different arrows. Something with no inputs produces nothing on its own; it only becomes productive once the things that consume its output exist and return something to it.

So the thing you need is a CYCLE: the smallest set whose members feed each other with no external input required. Not the most important sector — the shortest closed loop. In practice that loop is nearly always unglamorous and involves food, water, and somebody keeping time, because those are the three that everything else quietly assumes. The same shape appears in the fourteen terraforming profiles. The envelope has ten downstream dependents — the most of anything on the board — and its own status is only "derived". the test

The window numbersettled

583.92 days between one departure and the next. Nothing you can spend shortens the wait.

The window is the synodic period — how long it takes Earth and Venus to return to the same relative position — and it comes straight out of the two orbital periods with nothing else in it:

1 ÷ (1÷224.701 − 1÷365.256) = 583.9 days. Venus goes round in 224.701 days, Earth in 365.256, and the difference in angular rate is the whole calculation. Call it 584 days, nineteen and a bit months, one year seven months.

The windows themselves nearly repeat on a longer cycle, and this is the useful part for anybody planning more than one: five synodic periods is 2,919.6 days, eight Earth years is 2,922.0, and thirteen Venus years is 2,921.1. All three land within two and a half days of each other. So the pattern of departures you get in one eight-year block is very close to the pattern you get in the next, which means a schedule can be written once and reused, and the drift is small enough to absorb.

The operational fact is that this is a deadline nobody negotiated. Most schedules can be bought forward with more money, more people, or a different contractor. This one is set by two orbital periods, and a launch that is not ready waits 584 days — not for a review, not for a decision, for geometry.

the test

Ask what would shorten it. If the answer offered is budget, headcount, or a better vendor, that is a different constraint being described. The only thing that moves a departure inside the window is propellant, and what it buys is a worse transfer, not an earlier arrival.

The passage numbersettled

146 days out, on the minimum-energy path. The fast way there is not the straight one.

The minimum-energy transfer is half an ellipse with Earth's orbit at one end and Venus's at the other, and its duration falls out of one line of Kepler:

semi-major axis = (1.0000 + 0.7233) ÷ 2 = 0.86165 AU. Orbital period = 0.86165^1.5 = 0.7998 years = 292.1 days. You fly half of it: 146 days. About 3.5 km/s from low Earth orbit to get on it.

IT IS A STRAIGHT SHOT AND IT IS NOT A STRAIGHT LINE, and the difference is why it is quick. You are not driving to Venus; you are letting go and falling toward the Sun, and Venus arrives at the far end of the fall. A straight line at constant speed would need thrust the whole way and propellant to match. The ellipse costs one burn and then nothing.

For scale, since the Moon is the only trip anybody has a feel for: the Moon is 384,400 km, and Venus at its closest is about 99 Earth–Moon distances. Apollo took roughly three days to the Moon; at that average speed Venus would take the better part of a year. It takes 146 days instead, because falling beats driving.

The three-to-five months quoted in the ships' logs is the same number with the variants included — 146 days is 4.8 months on the minimum-energy path, and the shorter figures are bought with propellant.

the test

Compute the transfer period from the two semi-major axes and halve it. If a quoted passage time is much shorter than that, it is not a minimum-energy transfer, and the difference is propellant somebody has to launch. Ask for the delta-v alongside the duration; a duration on its own is not a plan.

The light delay numbersettled

2.2 minutes at closest, 14.3 at furthest, one way. There is no live conversation with Venus.

Venus and Earth run between about 39.5 and 258 million km apart on actual approaches — the geometric extremes are 38.2 and 261.0, from Earth's perihelion minus Venus's aphelion and both aphelia added, but the orbits are not aligned to deliver those often.

Divide by 299,792.458 km/s: 39.5 million gives 131.8 seconds, 2.20 minutes. 258 million gives 860.6 seconds, 14.34 minutes. Those are one way. A question and its answer is 4.4 minutes at best and 28.7 at worst.

THAT NUMBER IS AN OPERATIONS CONSTRAINT BEFORE IT IS A COMMUNICATIONS ONE. Any procedure whose correct response has to arrive in under about four and a half minutes cannot be run from Earth, at any point in the cycle, ever. Not with better equipment and not with more staff. It has to be decided at the far end, which means the authority to decide it has to be there before the situation is.

The Moon is 1.28 light-seconds. Apollo could be talked through a problem in real time and repeatedly was. Venus cannot be, and every procedure written on the assumption that somebody can be asked needs rewriting for a place where they cannot.

The practical shape of this is already in the yard. Signals are built to be read by weight before colour resolves, because a code for a channel with no back-channel has to survive being read badly. The bridge recomputes the derived figures from an incoming manifest rather than believing them, because there is nobody to query about a discrepancy inside a quarter of an hour.

The relay mechanismsettled

A relay race, not a haul. The baton crosses the whole distance; no runner does. Nothing is lost at the exchange and the runner goes home and starts again.

A haul is one carrier from origin to destination, and its cost is the whole distance every time. A relay is stations and handoffs, each leg sized to what that leg can actually do, and the baton is the only thing that goes the whole way.

THE EXCHANGE IS THE INSTRUMENT AND NOTHING MAY BE DROPPED IN IT. In a relay race the exchange zone is the only place the baton can be lost, which is why it is the part that gets practised — the running is fitness, the handoff is skill. Same here: a leg that arrives is not the achievement, a leg that transfers cleanly is. Convoy releases in pairs and the markup does not offer half a pair, which is a stronger guarantee than a rule saying not to.

AND THE RUNNER GOES HOME AND STARTS OFF RIGHT. The hull is not the baton. It hands over and cycles back on its own clock, which is a different clock from the cargo's — outbound is 146 days, the return is set by the same synodic geometry rather than by the ship, so a hull either waits at the far end for its alignment or spends propellant to shorten the wait. Either way the cargo does not wait with it. That is the whole reason to relay rather than shuttle: the baton keeps moving while the runners reset.

The legs are not equal and are not meant to be. Earth departure carries sixteen states and can refuse. The middle leg carries eight and can only pace. Arrival carries four and can only receive. Each is further from the authority that can say no, and that is the light delay drawn as a chain rather than described — by the time a decision could be sent, the leg it applied to has already run.

The practical payoff is scheduling. A haul has to catch the 584-day window whole. A relay only needs each leg to catch its own, so the cadence becomes a pipeline with more than one thing in flight at a time rather than a gate that opens twice in three years.

the test

Ask who is responsible for the cargo at the midpoint. If it is the same party as at departure, it is a haul with extra stops and you are paying the whole distance. In a relay the answer is a station, with different authority from the one before it, and there is a defined moment when responsibility changed hands.

The baton conceptsettled

The only object in the system whose journey is the point. The hull is not the baton, and confusing the two is the commonest error there is.

Everything else in a relay exists to move it. The runners, the stations, the schedule, the exchange zones — none of those are going anywhere in the sense that matters; they cycle, they reset, they go home. One object crosses the whole distance and it is not any of them.

MOST SYSTEMS CANNOT NAME THEIR BATON, and that is why they optimise runners. Hull utilisation, desk throughput, cases disposed, seats filled, uptime — every one of those is a measurement of a carrier, and a system can improve all of them at once while the baton sits at a station. The relay reports one thing for exactly this reason: where the baton is. Not how the hull did.

THE BATON HAS NO STATE EXCEPT POSITION. It does not accumulate merit from a clean handoff or wear from a late one. A baton that waited a full 584-day window at an exchange and a baton that went straight through are the same baton when they reach Venus, differing only in when. That is fungibility applied to cargo, and it is the whole reason a held baton is not a loss: held and handed produce an identical object at the far end.

The only place a relay fails, and the only place authority changes. For the length of it the object has either two owners or none.

A relay does not fail in the middle of a leg. The legs are long, visible and measured, and they are not where anything goes wrong. It fails in the seconds where the baton is in nobody's hand — and that is where the design attention belongs and almost never goes, because the exchange is short and looks like nothing.

THE HAZARD IS THAT RESPONSIBILITY DOES NOT TRANSFER AT AN INSTANT. It transfers over an interval, and inside the interval there are only two possibilities and both are bad. Two owners: each is entitled to assume the other has it, and both assumptions are reasonable. No owner: nobody is watching and nobody is wrong for not watching. Every dropped baton in any system is one of those two, and neither is a competence failure — they are the arithmetic of an undefined boundary.

SO THE FIX IS A DEFINED MOMENT, checkable after the fact by somebody who was not there. The injector figuresettled

What the operator of an automated thing actually is. Two verbs: start it, and stop it. Steering is not on the list.

Automation does not remove the operator. It changes the operator's verbs, and the change is bigger than it looks: a continuous control surface — many small corrections, made constantly, each one cheap and each one revisable — collapses into a handful of discrete injections. Fewer decisions, and every one of them heavier, because each is now committed for the whole duration of a run.

THE MIDDLE IS THE PART THAT CHANGED. In the relay you cannot correct once it is flying, and the reason is physical rather than a design choice: by arrival your picture of the hull is 4.94 minutes old and anything you send is 4.94 minutes from landing. Most automated systems are in the same position and disguise it. The operator believes intervention is available, and it is, and it is useless — by the time a problem is perceived the system has already acted on it several times. Calling yourself an injector is not a diminishment. It is admitting the thing that was true anyway.

SO THE SKILL IS TIMING, NOT INTERVENTION. The relay has exactly one decision in it and it is when to leave: at the top of the window the passage is 146 days, and two hundred days off the top it is 236. Ninety days, bought or lost, in the one press you actually get. An injector who spends their attention wishing they could steer is spending it on the one thing they do not have, and not on the one thing they do.

AND THE STOP IS NOT A CONTROL, IT IS A REFUSAL. Shutdown does not adjust, tune, pause or reset — it stops, and nothing it stops is undone. That is a different verb from adjustment and the difference is the diagnostic: if mid-run adjustment is on the operator's list, the thing is not automated, it is ASSISTED. Assisted is a perfectly good design. It simply has different failure modes, needs different training, and should not be described with the other word.

The same shape is already elsewhere in this yard. The automat has four lights and one stop, and the stop resets nothing. The contract desk can complete, escalate, or burn — three verbs, and the third one only refuses. The chain's three legs carry sixteen, eight and four states, strictly decreasing, and the arrival leg can only receive. None of those were built from this entry; the entry is what they have in common.

the test

Count the operator's verbs and check whether mid-run adjustment is one of them. If it is, the system is assisted rather than automated and somebody is still flying it. If it is not, find the last moment a decision could have changed the outcome — that moment is the operator's entire job, and if the interface gives it the same weight as everything else, the interface is wrong.

Smart contracts mechanismcontested

Code that guarantees execution, not truth. It enforces the terms it was handed and never asks whether they are fair.

What a smart contract actually promises is narrow and worth stating plainly: given these inputs, this code runs, deterministically, and everyone can check that it ran. That is a real guarantee and it is the only one. It says nothing about whether the inputs were true, whether the terms were reasonable, or whether the parties understood them.

Everything the contract knows about the world arrived through somebody's hand. That is the oracle problem and it is not a rough edge to be engineered away — it is structural. A contract that pays on delivery cannot see a crate; it sees a signature asserting a crate. So the discretion did not disappear when the code went on-chain. It moved to whoever signs, and to whoever wrote the terms, and to whoever gets to say what the words in the terms refer to.

THIS ENTRY IS MARKED CONTESTED BECAUSE THIS REPOSITORY HAS SAID TWO THINGS. One position: there are no blockers, smart contracts prevent syndication — if allocation is in code and no human chooses who receives what, the classic move of matriculating funds upward and promoting within has nowhere to happen. The other, from theory.md: a syndicate's only move is de-fungibilisation, and de-fungibilisation happens in the noun, off-chain, before anything reaches a contract at all.

The reconciliation, as far as it goes: A CONTRACT CAN CLOSE THE DISCRETIONARY PATH AND CANNOT CLOSE THE SEMANTIC ONE. Where the allocation is genuinely mechanical, the first position holds and it holds strongly — that route really is shut, and shutting it is not nothing. But the rename is upstream of the contract, and a contract handed the renamed thing will execute against it faithfully and irreversibly, which is worse than a clerk who might have hesitated. Neither position is wrong about its own half and the yard has not shown a board where both halves are in view at once. Until it does, this stays contested.

One scope rule this yard did adopt and does keep: SMART CONTRACTS ARE FOR WALLET TRANSACTIONS. Not membership, not governance, not adjudication. The moment a contract is asked to decide who belongs, it has become a bench — and a bench that cannot be appealed is strictly worse than one that can, because appeal is the only mechanism anybody has ever built for the case where the rule was right and the outcome was wrong.

And a contract is only as good as the checks somebody paid for. contract.html gives that cost a name: gas. A check nobody paid for is a check nobody ran, and an unpaid check looks exactly like a passed one from the outside.

the test

Follow the value and find the last point where a human chose. If there is one, the contract automated the disbursement and not the decision — syndication lives at the choice, not the transfer. If there genuinely is none, stop looking there and ask two questions instead: who wrote the terms, and who gets to say what the nouns in the terms refer to.

The harvest guarantee mechanismworking

A guild is a bank to an amateur and an amateur farm to a journeyman. The one term that breaks it says the contributor keeps harvesting what they brought.

A GUILD IS TWO INSTRUMENTS WEARING ONE NAME, and which one you are holding depends entirely on where you stand in it.

TO AN AMATEUR IT IS A BANK. You arrive with no tools, no customers, no standing and no way to get any of the three, and the guild fronts you all of it against output you have not produced yet. That is credit, it is genuinely useful, and pretending otherwise is how critiques of these structures get dismissed by the people inside them — because the people inside them are correct that they were helped.

TO A JOURNEYMAN THE SAME INSTITUTION IS AN AMATEUR FARM. Once you have the skill, what the guild supplies is no longer capital, it is a standing crop of people who have to work under somebody and it may as well be you. The word for managing a living thing for yield is HUSBANDRY, and it is the same word for caring for it. That is not a coincidence of English and it is the entire ambiguity: the man husbanding the crop is not lying when he says he looks after it.

WHICH IS WHY THE SHAPE IS STABLE, AND THIS IS THE PART THE REST OF THIS BASE COULD NOT EXPLAIN. Both positions profit. The amateur needs the bank and will defend it. The journeyman needs the farm and will defend it. Nobody inside has an incentive to break it, so it does not need enforcement, secrecy or bad faith to persist for centuries — and it explains why spotting a syndicate from the inside is so hard. The people being farmed are being banked at the same moment by the same institution, and both halves are true, and the true half is the one they will cite.

THE TERM THAT BREAKS IT IS NARROW. Not ownership of the commons, not a vote, not a share of the whole: a guaranteed claim on the stream produced by what YOU brought, which cannot be voted away, diluted, reassigned on somebody's promotion, or made to lapse by leaving. You continue to harvest your own contribution for as long as it produces.

AND IT IS THE RIGHT USE OF A SMART CONTRACT, precisely because it stays inside the scope rule this base already keeps: it is a wallet transaction. It decides no membership, no governance and no dispute. It says one thing — this address receives this stream, and no majority reassigns it — which is the one kind of statement code can actually enforce without becoming a bench that cannot be appealed.

WHAT IT REMOVES IS THE FARM AND NOT THE BANK. The journeyman's return stops arriving from the amateur's contribution and starts arriving from their own, so the crop is gone; but the amateur still gets the tools, the standing and the customers, because that half was never the extraction. A structure that keeps the credit and loses the harvest is a guild that has stopped being a syndicate without anybody having to be expelled from it.

THE HONEST LIMIT: IT DOES NOT STOP THE RENAME. Somebody can still redefine what counts as 'what you brought' — reclassify the contribution, split it, call it a work for hire, call it derivative. The contract enforces the transfer and not the definition, the definition sits upstream of the contract, and that is the contested entry arriving again in a different coat. Close the discretionary path and the semantic one is still open.

Then ask the harder question, because it is where this will actually be attacked: who defines what the newcomer brought? The contract enforces the transfer and not the definition, and a guarantee over a category somebody else controls is a guarantee over nothing.

ORGANIC CARBON — not there, and not shipped either. It is grown. The air is 96.5% CO2 and photosynthesis is the machine that turns that into soil organic matter. You do not import humus; you import the thing that makes humus and then wait.

THE LIVING FRACTION — this, and only this, has to come from Earth. A gram of healthy soil carries on the order of a billion bacteria and a million fungal propagules, so a kilogram is 1e12 organisms and a hundred kilograms is 1e14. The shipping manifest for the biology of an entire forest is a culture collection that fits inside a single window's 11,400 kg with room for everything else.

SURTSEY IS THE EXPERIMENT, AND IT WAS RUN ACCIDENTALLY. A volcanic island surfaced off Iceland in November 1963 — sterile basalt, no soil, nothing alive on it. The first colonists were not plants: they were bacteria, fungi and microorganisms. The first vascular plant, a sea rocket, was found in 1965. Ten species by the end of the first decade. Seventy-four recorded between 1965 and 2015, of which thirty-nine had established viable populations by 2013.

AND THE THING THAT UNLOCKED IT WAS BIRDS — WHICH IS WHY THE ANALOGY BREAKS. A gull colony formed on Surtsey after 1985, bird dispersal became the principal pathway, and the guano amended the site enough that wind-dispersed species could establish from 1990. But the gulls FED IN THE NORTH ATLANTIC and defecated on the island. That is an allochthonous subsidy: the nutrients were imported, the birds were the pump, and the ocean was the reservoir. A sealed deck has no ocean and no outside. Animals on it eat on-system and excrete on-system, which makes them redistributors and not importers, and their net nutrient addition is exactly zero at any species count. Surtsey's SEQUENCE holds — microbes first, then plants, then animals. Surtsey's MECHANISM does not, and an earlier version of this entry said it did.

SO WHAT ACTUALLY IMPORTS NUTRIENTS INTO A CLOSED PLATFORM IS NOT AN ANIMAL. Nitrogen fixation, which is where the 4.3x atmospheric nitrogen finally cashes out: rhizobia, Frankia and free-living cyanobacteria pulling N2 out of air that holds more of it than Earth's, needing no ocean and no bird. Basalt weathering, releasing P, K, Ca and Mg as fresh rock breaks down — finite but large, and the reason the mineral fraction being basalt matters more than a parent-material footnote. And carbon fixation straight out of a 96.5% CO2 atmosphere. All three are microbial or geological. None of them is a bird.

the test

Sort the soil into its four fractions — mineral, nitrogen, organic carbon, living — and ask which of them actually has to arrive. If the answer is 'all of it', somebody has costed the mineral fraction and forgotten that carbon is grown from the air and the living fraction is an inoculum measured in kilograms. Then ask the question that catches the subtler error: is the system OPEN? Every terrestrial analogue you are reasoning from has an outside, and if yours does not, any mechanism in the analogue that imports nutrients is doing nothing in yours.

Fish in a bag conceptworking

A sealed platform has no outside. Nothing enters, nothing leaves, and every ecological analogue drawn from an open system quietly assumes one.

A goldfish in a bag has a fixed amount of everything. It does not get new water. Its waste does not go anywhere. Whatever was in the bag at the start is what there is, rearranged.

A 9,000 square mile deck at 55 km is a bag. There is no sea to feed in, no watershed draining in from upstream, no dust blowing off a continent, no migratory anything arriving with a gut full of somewhere else. Every terrestrial ecosystem anybody would reason from has all four of those and does not mention them, because on Earth an outside is not a feature, it is the condition.

SO THE FAILURE MODE IS NOT SHORTAGE, IT IS IMMOBILISATION. In a closed system the nutrients do not run out — they get locked into forms nothing available can use. Phosphorus into recalcitrant organic matter, nitrogen into lignin, carbon into wood that nothing on the platform can decompose at the rate it is being made. The bag stays full and the fish still starves.

WHICH REASSIGNS THE ANIMALS. On an open system, colonial waterbirds are a nutrient PUMP — they feed in one place and deposit in another, and the whole point is that the two places are different. In a bag there is only one place. Animals there are TURNOVER: they eat matter that is locking up, excrete it in a labile form something else can take, and keep the cycling rate high enough that the standing stock does not become the sequestered stock. That is a real and necessary job and it is not the job anybody assigns them.

AND IT SETS A DIFFERENT DESIGN QUESTION. The question is not 'what do we bring in' — after the inoculum, almost nothing. It is 'what is the residence time of a phosphorus atom', and the answer had better be years rather than centuries. A bag with slow turnover is a peat bog: enormous nutrient stock, almost none of it available, and a system that looks healthy in inventory and is starving in flux.

the test

Name the outside. Point at where a nutrient enters the system and where one leaves. If you cannot point at either, stop reasoning from open-system analogues — and then stop asking about stock and start asking about residence time, because in a bag the number that kills you is how long an atom stays locked up, not how many of them there are.

CSS reads the fragment through :target. It cannot read the query. Nothing can. So that is where the script layer lives.

A document has exactly one :target at a time, and the fragment is the only durable state a scriptless page has — it survives reload, it is in the bookmark, it goes in a message, Back is undo. The query string is the other half of the same address and it is invisible to the style layer completely.

That asymmetry is not a limitation to work around, it is a channel. automat.js and devmode.js are wired into every page in this yard and are inert unless the address carries ?run or ?dev. Open a board plainly and nothing runs and the stylesheet decides everything, exactly as before. Open it with a query and a script is pressing the controls — and the two layers cannot collide, because they read different halves of the same URL.

The claim was never that there is no script file. It was that the logic is the cascade, and that is intact and now checkable from inside the page.

Syndication conceptsettled

Two or more, acting as one, sitting where everything has to pass. Not one — one is a monopoly. It taxes the rate, never the stock.

YOU ALONE CANNOT BE A SYNDICATE, and that is not a nicety of usage, it is the whole structure. A single party whose removal disconnects two others is a cut vertex, and a cut vertex is a monopoly or a bottleneck. A syndicate is a COMBINATION: a set of two or more whose members are individually removable and collectively not. They are a cut SET, and they behave as one.

The consequence is the useful part and it is not obvious. Remove any single member of a syndicate and nothing happens, because the others cover. Which means the standard test for centralisation — delete one component and see whether anything else moves — is STRUCTURALLY BLIND to syndication. It will return a clean result on an arrangement that is entirely captured. To find a syndicate you have to delete combinations, and combinations are where nobody looks because there are so many more of them.

So the procedure has two halves. Find the minimal sets whose removal disconnects: sets where the whole set cuts and no proper subset of it does. Then ask whether the members are CORRELATED — do they price, refuse and time together? A minimal cut set of independent parties is just thin redundancy and it is fine. The same set moving in step is a syndicate, and no member of it has to know that.

NOW THE HALF THAT IS USUALLY GOT WRONG. Syndication is a hindrance and not a loss factor. It acts on the RATE at which you gain and never on the STOCK you already hold. A toll makes the road slower and the journey dearer; it does not reach into the cart. Everything a syndicate can do sits in that sentence — slow you, lengthen your path, price your passage, refuse you a turn — and not one of those subtracts what you already brought.

Which is why a base can run on a law that nothing ever goes down and still take this seriously. The two do not conflict. Being syndicated means climbing at a worse rate than you otherwise would have, forever, and seeing it requires comparing yourself against a counterfactual rather than against yesterday. That is exactly why it is invisible from inside, and it is the second reason to trust the structural test over the felt experience.

A party that takes what you already hold is not a syndicate. That is confiscation: a different mechanism with a different remedy. You can be robbed by somebody holding no position at all, and you can be thoroughly syndicated by people who never take a thing.

the test

Three ways to break this and they are not equally easy. FIRST, the cheap one: find a genuine syndicate that is a single party. If you can, the combination requirement is wrong and the word means something closer to bottleneck. SECOND: find a minimal cut set of two or more whose members demonstrably do NOT move together — independent pricing, independent refusals, independent timing — and which everybody nonetheless agrees is a syndicate. That would mean correlation is not required and the shape alone is enough. THIRD, and this is the load-bearing one: find a case where the set removes what was already earned rather than slowing what is earned next, and show it is syndication rather than confiscation wearing its coat. If that case stands, the hindrance-not-loss claim is false, and this entry and the +1 law cannot both survive it.
