The Fungus

Under the flower bed there is a market running on the most fungible commodity there is, and it is not an undifferentiated market. This page exists because the theory got it wrong by stopping at the unit.

The correction what the first draft missed

theory.md wrote fungibility as a property of units and stopped there: a bee is a bee, a pound is a pound, so the system has no individual loss condition and no scarcity rent. That is true and it is not the whole thing.

A fungible currency does not produce an undifferentiated system. It moves the discrimination from the commodity to the counterparty. You cannot tell one glucose from another, so nothing is decided about the sugar — and everything is decided about who sent it and what came back. That happens under every flower bed on this planet and it was measurable before anybody thought to look.

What actually moves counted from the panels, not declared

Carbon, downwardplant → fungus

Sugar. The most fungible substance in the whole system: one glucose is indistinguishable from another by any test, chemical or biological, and nothing about a molecule records where it came from or who paid for it.

Phosphorus, upwardfungus → plant

Also fungible. Also anonymous. Both directions of this trade are carried in commodities that cannot be branded, traced, or told apart — which by the argument in theory.md ought to make the market undifferentiated, and does not.

Reciprocal rewardthe finding

The plant sends more carbon to the fungal partners that deliver more phosphate, and the fungus sends more phosphate to the roots that deliver more carbon. Both sides discriminate, simultaneously, using commodities neither side can identify the origin of.

This was shown with isotope tracers — label the phosphorus, label the carbon, and watch where each one accumulates when a root is offered partners of different quality at once. The allocation follows the return. Neither party can inspect the goods, and both are nevertheless choosing.

Which is the correction. The discrimination did not disappear when the currency became fungible. It relocated. It stopped being about the thing and became entirely about the relationship, which is exactly what you would expect and precisely what the first draft failed to say.

The poor partner gets lessplant · poor partner

Sanctioned without a sanction. There is no adjudication here, no detection of cheating, no rule and no enforcement — the plant simply allocates in proportion to what arrives, and a partner returning less receives less. The mechanism is one line long and it does not require anybody to be caught.

Compare the wolves and the owls: a chamber needs quorum, procedure, a decisive denominator and a rule about abstention to get to an outcome this market reaches by arithmetic. The difference is that a plant is not trying to be fair. It is trying to be fed, and proportional allocation is the cheapest thing that works.

Bare soilnobody trading

No parties, no flows. The phosphorus is still in the ground and still out of reach, which is the state this whole arrangement exists to get out of: not a shortage of the mineral, a shortage of anything with hyphae fine enough to fetch it.

Peach where fungible parts stop being the whole story

Peach is not a molecule. A great deal of it is carried by gamma-decalactone, and every molecule of gamma-decalactone is identical to every other molecule of it — perfectly fungible, no exceptions, nothing to distinguish. And the flavour is still not that compound. It is a ratio: lactones against esters against aldehydes, over a sugar-to-acid balance, in a texture, at a temperature.

the unitone lactoneFungible without qualification. Any molecule substitutes for any other.
the rationot fungibleChange the proportions and it is a different fruit, from identical parts.
the mediumnot fungibleSame compounds, wrong sugar-acid balance, and it reads as tinned.
the resultpeachA composition. Nothing in it is particular and the arrangement is.

This is the step the theory missed. Fungibility at the level of the unit says nothing whatever about the level above it. A system can be built entirely out of interchangeable parts and still produce results that are not interchangeable at all — which is true of a flavour, a forest, a chamber and a piece of work, and it is the reason a fungible medium is not the same thing as a flat world.

One fungus the largest organism anybody has found

In the Malheur National Forest in Oregon there is a single individual of Armillaria ostoyae covering roughly 2,385 acres — about 9.6 km², or 3.7 square miles. Not a colony and not a population: genetic testing says one organism, spreading underground as a continuous network, and estimates of its age start around two and a half thousand years.

and it isa pathogenArmillaria kills trees. That is how it got that big.

Say the last one plainly. The largest organism anybody has measured is a parasite, and the reason it covers three and a half square miles is that it has been killing the trees above it for millennia. The network under a wood is not automatically a commons or a kindness. Sometimes it is one animal eating a forest slowly, and the fact that it is beautiful and old does not make it a partnership.

And it inverts the market. Everything above this section describes trade between separate parties who discriminate by return. A single individual spanning the whole bed is not trading with itself — there is no counterparty, no reciprocity to measure, and no question of a poor partner. The same physical structure runs both arrangements, and you cannot tell which one you are looking at without genotyping it. That is the honest state of the field, and it is why the next section exists.

What is oversold and this page is not going to repeat it

The popular version of this — mother trees deliberately feeding seedlings, a forest sharing resources through a wood wide web — runs well ahead of what the papers support. Common mycorrhizal networks are real. The exchange above is real and measured. The claims about networks routinely redistributing resources for the benefit of seedlings were reviewed in 2023 by Karst, Jones and Hoeksema, who found the evidence considerably weaker than the retellings, and found citation drift: each repetition stated it more strongly than the source it cited.

Keep the trade. Drop the parable. It is the same failure as the moth at the candle, one level up — a description that was calibrated to a particular study and then carried into conditions it was never fitted for, behaving exactly as specified and being wrong the whole way.
