Signal Code · three lamps

Yellow, blue, green — the panel the cruiser lane runs on since red came off it. Eight states, and not one of them is a stop. no script
000
no signal

Dark

Worse here than on the four-lamp panel. With no red anywhere in the code, dark is the only state that resembles a stop — and it is not one. A hull showing nothing is not held; it is unreadable.

100
aspect

Slow

Under way and short of cruise. Something is in the way — the constrained setting, not the economical one.

010
aspect

Cruise

The setting a hull sits on for a long lane, and the only one meant to be held indefinitely. On this panel it is also the default: with no stop, something always has to be showing.

001
aspect

Full

Everything she has, for as long as it takes. A request rather than a state — nothing crosses ten light years green.

110
transition

Rejoining cruise

Coming up off slow onto the economical setting. The obstruction is cleared and she is settling back onto the lane.

101
transition

Working up

Slow, going to full, skipping cruise. Power is arriving and is not there yet — the interval where she is committed but not capable.

011
transition

Released

At cruise with standing authority to go full without asking. A permission rather than a movement, and the only one left on this panel.

111
all lit

Test, or everything at once

Every lamp answering. Struck to prove the panel before departure; otherwise it means everything is happening simultaneously, which at a signal panel is the same message as nothing being legible.

Removing one lamp halves the code — 2⁴ to 2³ — and the row goes from 1 4 6 4 1 to 1 3 3 1. But the loss is not spread evenly. One aspect goes and three transitions go with it, because every pair that included the missing lamp disappears too. And the whole degraded category vanishes: on a three-lamp panel, three lit is "all lit", so there is no longer any state that means something is unavailable. A panel that cannot report its own faults is not a smaller code. It is a different one.

Which lamp you drop four possible panels

loses red

No stop

Three speeds and no brake. Nothing in the code can hold a hull, and nothing can say that it should be held — the order simply does not exist any more.

what the lane runs on
loses yellow

No slow

She can stop and she can cross, but she has no steerage speed below cruise — so she cannot creep, and cannot come alongside anything. A hull that can sail and can stop and can do nothing careful.

loses blue

No cruise

Stop, slow and full, with nothing in between that can be held for a long lane. She arrives — expensively, and needing more fuel than she was budgeted for.

loses green

No full

Stop, slow, cruise. She can do everything except sprint, which is the cheapest of the four losses — a plant down a bank rather than a capability gone.

The four are not interchangeable. Dropping green costs a sprint. Dropping red costs the ability to stop — and worse, the ability to order a stop, which is not the same thing and is the more serious of the two. A hull can always coast to rest eventually; a code with no red in it has no way for anyone else to say when.

Scale for the record

Three lamps hold 8 states. The number of possible functions from those states to a three-valued output is 3⁸ = 6,561 — the same figure the cruiser lane shows for eight hulls each set to one of three aspects, which is a coincidence of arithmetic and not a relationship: one is 3 to the power of 8 states, the other is 3 to the power of 8 hulls. Worth noting precisely because it would be easy to mistake for a connection.
